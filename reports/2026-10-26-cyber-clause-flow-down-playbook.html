Cyber Clause Flow-Down Playbook: Which Clauses Go to Subcontractors, With What Exceptions

Key takeaways

  • 7012 goes down "without alteration." DFARS 252.204-7012(m) must be included in subcontracts for operationally critical support or involving covered defense information, including commercial subcontracts.
  • No sub award without a Basic Assessment. DFARS 252.204-7020(g) bars a prime from awarding a subcontract subject to 7012 unless the subcontractor has completed at least a Basic Assessment within the last 3 years.
  • COTS is the main exception. 7020, 7021 and FAR 52.204-21 exclude commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) items. 7012 has no COTS carve-out in paragraph (m).
  • Prohibition clauses go everywhere. FAR 52.204-23, -25, -27 and -30 flow to all subcontracts, including commercial ones. FAR 52.244-6 lists them as mandatory in commercial subcontracts.
  • CMMC levels flow by data type. 32 CFR 170.23 sets the minimum level for a subcontractor. With Phase 2 suspended, only Level 1 (Self) and Level 2 (Self) may currently be required.
  • A consolidation is proposed. FAR Case 2026-001 would fold 52.204-23, -25, -27 and -30 into one clause, 52.240-3, and move 52.204-21 to 52.240-5. Nothing is final yet.

The table restates the subcontract paragraph of each clause. It does not replace reading the clause in your contract, which may carry a different date or a deviation.

7012: the one clause you may not edit

DFARS 252.204-7012(m)(1) requires the prime to include the clause, including paragraph (m), in subcontracts for operationally critical support or where performance will involve covered defense information.

The prime must decide whether the information a subcontractor needs keeps its identity as covered defense information. If necessary, it should consult the contracting officer.

Paragraph (m)(2) adds two duties the prime must impose:

  • Variance requests. The subcontractor notifies the prime, or next higher tier, when it asks the contracting officer to vary from a NIST SP 800-171 requirement.
  • Incident report number. The subcontractor gives the prime, or next higher tier, the incident report number DoD assigns automatically.

7020: check the sub's score before you sign

DFARS 252.204-7020(g)(1) flows the substance of the clause to all subcontracts, including commercial ones, excluding COTS.

Paragraph (g)(2) is an award gate. The prime "shall not award" a subcontract subject to 800-171 under 7012 unless the subcontractor has completed, within the last 3 years, at least a Basic NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment for relevant systems.

DOJ's settlement record shows suppliers are in scope. Swiss Automation, described by DOJ as a supplier, paid $421,234 in December 2025 to resolve cyber-fraud allegations.

7021 and 32 CFR 170.23: flow the right CMMC level

DFARS 252.204-7021(d)(1)(ii) tells the contractor to consult 32 CFR 170.23 "and flow down the correct CMMC level." Paragraph (f) flows the clause to subcontracts that involve FCI or CUI, excluding COTS.

32 CFR 170.23(a) sets the subcontractor minimums:

  • FCI only. Level 1 (Self).
  • CUI. Level 2 (Self) at minimum.
  • CUI, prime at Level 2 (C3PAO). Level 2 (C3PAO) at minimum.
  • CUI, prime at Level 3 (DIBCAC). Level 2 (C3PAO) at minimum.

Under the July 13, 2026 implementing memo 26-P-1023, a solicitation or contract may currently designate only Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self). The higher rows of 170.23 therefore have no current prime requirement to attach to.

FAR prohibitions flow to every tier

The FAR supply-chain prohibition clauses are broader than the DFARS cyber clauses:

  • 52.204-23 (Kaspersky). Paragraph (d) flows to all subcontracts, including commercial.
  • 52.204-25 (Section 889). Paragraph (e) flows to all subcontracts, excluding paragraph (b)(2). Paragraph (d) requires a report within one business day of identifying covered equipment, to dibnet.dod.mil for DoD contracts.
  • 52.204-27 (ByteDance). Flows to all subcontracts.
  • 52.204-30 (FASCSA orders). Paragraph (e) flows to all subcontracts, excluding paragraph (c)(1).

FAR 52.244-6(c)(1) (OCT 2025) lists all four, plus 52.204-21 where its own flow-down applies, among clauses the contractor "shall insert" in commercial subcontracts.

FAR 52.204-21: basic safeguarding where FCI sits

FAR 52.204-21(c) flows to subcontracts, including commercial ones other than COTS, "in which the subcontractor may have Federal contract information residing in or transiting through its information system."

Raytheon and Nightwing paid $8.4 million in May 2025. DOJ's release said the allegations covered both DFARS 252.204-7012 and FAR 52.204-21.

Cloud providers are a flow-down too

Two clauses reach cloud services:

  • 7012(b)(2)(ii)(D). An external cloud provider used for covered defense information must meet security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline and comply with 7012 paragraphs (c) through (g).
  • 252.239-7010(l). This clause flows to all subcontracts that involve or may involve cloud services, including commercial services.

In the ILC Dover settlement (Nov 3, 2026), DOJ alleged the company stored CUI with a third-party cloud provider that did not meet the FedRAMP Moderate-equivalent and incident duties.

What FAR Case 2026-001 would change

The June 23, 2026 proposed rule would rebuild FAR Part 40. Its preamble says it would "consolidate five separate provisions into one provision" and "seven separate clauses to one clause."

  • 52.240-3. Would absorb 52.204-23, -25, -27, -28 and -30, 52.225-13 and 52.240-1.
  • 52.240-5. Would replace 52.204-21, Basic Safeguarding.
  • 52.240-7. A new CUI clause, absorbing FAR Case 2017-016 (CUI).
  • One timeline. Disclosure and reporting would standardize at 72 hours from discovery, replacing the one-business-day 889 report.

These are proposals. Until a final rule or class deviation takes effect, the clauses in your contract govern.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Price the sub base. Teaming partners handling CUI need a current SPRS Basic Assessment before award.
  • Name CUI in the teaming agreement. The prime must decide whether information keeps its identity as covered defense information, so define who gets what early.
  • Do not rely on COTS for 7012. Paragraph (m) has no COTS exception. Its trigger is the information and the support provided.

Compliance cost

  • Template the clause set. One subcontract template per tier, CUI, FCI-only and COTS, reduces errors.
  • Collect evidence at onboarding. SPRS score date, CMMC level where applicable, cloud provider status, and 889 representations.
  • Keep the edit out of 7012. Only the parties may be changed.

Execution

  • Set up upward reporting. Require subs to pass the DoD incident report number and variance requests up the chain.
  • Re-check at options. A sub's 3-year assessment can expire mid-performance.
  • Plan for renumbering. If Part 40 is finalized, subcontract templates will need new clause numbers.

Watch next

  • Final FAR Part 40 rule. No final rule for FAR Case 2026-001 was found in the Federal Register as of Oct 26, 2026.
  • CMMC review outcome. Further guidance after the 60-day review had not been published in the official sources opened.
  • DFARS conforming changes. No DFARS rule conforming 7012 to the proposed FAR 52.240-7 was found.
