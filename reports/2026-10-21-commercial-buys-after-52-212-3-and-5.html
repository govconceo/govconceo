Commercial Buys Without 52.212-3 and 52.212-5: The Government Picks the Clauses, and Off-List Clauses Need a Deviation

Key takeaways

  • The omnibus pair goes. FAR Case 2026-003 would remove 52.212-3 (Offeror Representations and Certifications) and 52.212-5 (statutes and executive orders). The preamble says they "provide incomplete lists."
  • The Government carries the burden. The rule "would also shift the burden of determining which provisions and clauses ... are applicable ... from the offeror or contractor to the Government."
  • Off-list clauses need a deviation. Adding a clause not prescribed for commercial buys would require an individual or class deviation under FAR Part 1. Today, FAR 12.301(d) gives contracting officers broad discretion.
  • Three clauses stay. 52.212-1, -2 and -4 remain, streamlined. The Comptroller General audit clause in 52.212-5(d) moves to 52.212-4(t).
  • Part 12 becomes a one-stop shop. Simplified procedures for commercial buys up to $9 million ($15 million for specified emergencies) and micro-purchase rules move into Part 12.
  • Annual reps change too. FAR Case 2026-001 would remove 52.204-8. Only entity-level reps would stay in SAM; procurement-specific ones would move into solicitations.

Findings

Why the omnibus pair is going

The omnibus provision and clause list provisions and clauses that might apply to a commercial buy. Offerors and contractors check which apply.

Under the proposal, the contracting officer, "or an agency clause logic system," would rely on updated prescriptions to decide which provisions and clauses go into a commercial solicitation or contract.

What stays, what moves

The preamble describes the following clause changes:

  • Kept. 52.212-1 (Instructions to Offerors), 52.212-2 (Evaluation) and 52.212-4 (Contract Terms and Conditions). 52.212-1 and 52.212-4 get plain-language edits and streamlining.
  • Removed. 52.212-3 and 52.212-5.
  • Moved. The statutory Comptroller General examination of records in 52.212-5(d) would move to 52.212-4(t).
  • Form change. Block 27 of the SF 1449 would drop its references to 52.212-3 and 52.212-5.

The preamble says these changes are "expected to substantially reduce the number of provisions and clauses included in commercial acquisitions."

Discretion narrows: adding a clause takes a deviation

Current FAR 12.301(d) gives the contracting officer "broad discretion to incorporate provisions and clauses not listed," according to the preamble.

If the proposal is finalized, an agency would need "an individual or class deviation in accordance with FAR part 1" to put a provision or clause not prescribed for commercial products or services into a commercial solicitation or contract.

Tailoring and waivers

The proposal would let agencies waive or tailor commercial provisions and clauses that do not implement a statute or executive order. The agency must find them inconsistent with customary commercial practice.

  • No addendum required. Tailoring would not have to be by addendum, but solicitations and contracts must "clearly indicate" tailoring (proposed 12.205(c)(1)(ii)).
  • Class waivers reported. Class waivers go to the Chair of the Civilian Agency Acquisition Council for civilian agencies, or the Director of Defense Acquisition Regulations System for defense.

The model deviation already lists clauses individually

Acquisition.gov's model Part 12 deviation, issued Aug 14, 2025 and updated Apr 20, 2026, already uses tables in 12.205 rather than an omnibus list:

  • Table 12-2. Provisions to include as prescribed. Examples include 52.204-7 (SAM registration), new 52.204-90 (Offeror Identification), and 52.209-12 (Certification Regarding Tax Matters).
  • Table 12-3. Clauses to include as prescribed. Examples include 52.203-13 (Code of Business Ethics), 52.204-13 (SAM maintenance), and new 52.204-91 (Contractor identification).

Each entry in the tables is tagged with its source, such as statute, executive order or "Other." Agencies that adopted the deviation may already issue commercial solicitations in this form.

Part 12 absorbs simplified and micro-purchase procedures

The preamble describes four consolidations into Part 12:

  • Publicizing. Requirements move from Part 5 to subpart 12.2. Proposed 12.202(b) would generally require a combined synopsis/solicitation on SAM.gov.
  • Simplified procedures. Procedures at or below the simplified acquisition threshold move from Part 13 to 12.201-1.
  • Micro-purchases. Procedures move from subpart 13.2 to new subpart 12.4.
  • Higher threshold. The statutory authority to use simplified procedures for commercial buys up to $9 million, or $15 million for certain emergencies or major disasters, moves from subpart 13.5 into Part 12.

Proposed 12.200 reminds contracting officers to check required sources under Part 8 first, including existing governmentwide contracts and BPAs.

What else stays in Part 12

The preamble lists non-statutory content that would be kept, including:

  • 12.106 and 12.107. Technical data and commercial software: acquire only what is "customarily provided to the public."
  • 12.203(a). Evaluation factors generally need not go beyond technical, price and past performance.
  • 12.304. Use the streamlined commercial termination procedures in 52.212-4, not Part 49.

Reps and certs: SAM keeps only entity-level answers

FAR Case 2026-001 would remove 52.204-8, Annual Representations and Certifications. The preamble says only entity-level representations "will remain in SAM." Procurement-specific ones "will be removed from SAM and used in solicitations as prescribed in the FAR."

The FAR Council "specifically invites feedback on potential impacts and changes in burden resulting from this substantial change in business process."

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Read the clause list in each RFP. Without an omnibus checklist, the solicitation itself is the full set.
  • Expect more reps in proposals. Procurement-specific representations would move from SAM into solicitations.
  • Bigger simplified buys. Commercial buys up to $9 million would be pushed toward simplified procedures, which favors fast, short quotes.

Compliance cost

  • Fewer clauses, if the estimate holds. The preamble expects a substantial reduction in clause count.
  • Rebuild your clause matrix. Compliance tools keyed to 52.212-5 paragraphs would need to map to individual prescriptions.
  • Watch for tailoring. Tailored clauses must be marked as such. Check every marked change.

Execution

  • Flow-down still applies. Commercial subcontract flow-downs sit in 52.244-6, which this case does not remove.
  • Audit access survives. GAO examination rights move to 52.212-4(t).
  • Ask about off-list clauses. Under the proposal, a clause outside the prescriptions needs a deviation, which is a fair question in Q&A.

Watch next

  • Final rule for FAR Case 2026-003. None as of Oct 21, 2026.
  • SAM changes. The 52.204-8 removal needs SAM system changes. No implementation date was found.
  • Agency clause-logic systems. The preamble names them as the tool for clause selection. No agency rollout dates were found in the sources opened.
