DoD Removes WuXi AppTec From the Section 1260H List, Without Prejudice to Relisting

Key takeaways

  • WuXi AppTec is delisted. A Department of Defense notice published November 10, 2026, removes WuXi AppTec Co., Ltd. (WuXi AppTec) from the Section 1260H list of “Chinese military companies” that was published June 10, 2026.
  • The stated reason is a court order. The notice says the removal is “in compliance with the order issued by the U.S. District Court of Columbia on August 7, 2026, granting WuXi AppTec’s Motion for Preliminary Injunction.”
  • The injunction language is specific. The notice says that order “enjoins the Department from enforcing, implementing, or otherwise giving effect to the Department’s designation of WuXi AppTec as a Chinese military company under Section 1260H.”
  • Relisting is not barred by this notice. The action is “without prejudice to relisting the entity on additional or supplementary grounds,” and the summary uses the same “without prejudice” wording for the company.
  • This notice does not authorize a general “can now do business” conclusion. The opened official texts describe listing, publication, delisting, and the injunction quoted above. They do not, in the pages opened for this report, set out a procurement clause, a contract prohibition, or a statement that contractors may now buy from or sell to the company.

Findings

DoD has published a delisting of WuXi AppTec

The Department of Defense, Office of the Secretary, published “Notice of Removal of Designated Chinese Military Company” on November 10, 2026, at 91 FR 72536. The named agency is the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense (Acquisition and Sustainment).

The action line is “Notice of removal of a company from the 1260H List of Chinese military companies operating in the United States.” The entity named in the supplementary information is “WuXi AppTec Co., Ltd. (WuXi AppTec).”

The notice uses both “Deputy Secretary of War” and “Deputy Secretary of Defense” for the official who decided the delisting. This report uses those titles as they appear and does not treat them as a single rewritten title.

What Section 1260H is, as these official notices state it

The November 10 notice, and the June 10, 2026 list notice it incorporates by reference, describe Section 1260H of the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, Public Law 116-283, as amended, and as “codified at 10 U.S.C. 113, note.”

The June 10, 2026 list notice, 91 FR 35189, uses the same annual-list duty and the same December 31, 2030 end date. Its parenthetical citation to the Public Law differs slightly from the November 10 wording and is not quoted as if the two sentences were identical.

The notices also state that Section 1260H(b)(2) requires the Secretary of Defense to publish the unclassified portion of the list in the Federal Register.

The June 10 notice additionally states that listed entities “qualify for designation as ‘Chinese military companies,’ are engaged in providing commercial services, manufacturing, producing, or exporting (as required by Section 1260H(g)(2)(B)(ii)), and operate directly or indirectly in the United States (as required by Section 1260H(a)).”

Those are the 1260H implications stated in the official texts opened for this report. This report does not add procurement-ban or “covered contractor” rules from statutes or clauses that were not opened here.

WuXi AppTec was on the June 10, 2026 list

The November 10 notice says WuXi AppTec is being removed from “the most recent Section 1260H List that was published in the Federal Register on June 10, 2026.” That June 10 notice, 91 FR 35189 (FR Doc. 2026-11571), was opened for this report. It includes this listing:

“WuXi AppTec Co., Ltd. (WuXi AppTec).” The June 10 notice states: “WuXi AppTec is indirectly owned by SASAC and is indirectly affiliated with SASTIND and the PLA (Section 1260H(g)(2)(B)(i)(I)).”

In the same June 10 notice, SASAC is identified as the State-Owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council, SASTIND as the State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defense, and PLA as the People’s Liberation Army. This report uses those expansions only as that notice uses them.

The delisting is tied to an injunction, and is without prejudice

The court name is quoted as it appears in the notice: “U.S. District Court of Columbia.” This report does not rewrite that caption.

The November 10 notice was dated November 5, 2026. Its Federal Register filing line is “Filed 11-9-26; 8:45 am.” It was published November 10, 2026.

The summary says the Deputy Secretary of War decided to delist the entity “without prejudice to relisting the company on additional or supplementary grounds.” The supplementary information uses the same “without prejudice” phrasing for “the entity.” The notice also says a Mandarin identification is in the regulations.gov docket DOD-2026-OS-2410.

The opened notices do not say the underlying litigation is finished. They do not say the company cannot be listed again. They do not state a new procurement clause, a license, or a general clearance to contract.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Teams that used the June 10, 2026 Section 1260H list in proposal or supply-chain screens should record this official delisting. They should not treat it as a blanket statement that all dealings with WuXi AppTec are now cleared.

  • Update the list you actually use. The official action is removal from the June 10, 2026 Section 1260H list published in the Federal Register.
  • Do not infer a general authorization. The opened notices do not say contractors may now do business with the company, or that other screening regimes do not apply.

Compliance cost

The notices do not quantify a cost. The compliance task is to align internal restricted-party processes with the published delisting and with the injunction language the notice quotes, without over-reading either one.

  • What changed. WuXi AppTec is removed from the list identified in the November 10 notice.
  • What remains open. Relisting on additional or supplementary grounds is expressly reserved. Other official lists or contract clauses were not opened for this report and are not cleared by this notice.

Execution

Screening files should cite 91 FR 72536 and keep the “without prejudice” and injunction language with the entry. Correspondence on the 1260H list is directed, in the notice, to the Department email address printed there.

  • Relisting risk is official, not speculative. The notice itself reserves relisting on additional or supplementary grounds.
  • The court order is as described, not reproduced. This report quotes the notice’s description of the August 7, 2026 preliminary injunction. It does not treat the court opinion as a separately opened source.

Watch next

  • A later 1260H notice. Section 1260H, as these notices state it, provides for additions or deletions not less frequently than annually, and this notice leaves relisting on additional or supplementary grounds open.
  • Any official text that states contracting consequences. If a later official FAR, DFARS, or statutory notice that was not opened here addresses 1260H and procurement, that text — not this delisting notice — would be the source for those rules.
  • The regulations.gov docket. The notice points readers to docket DOD-2026-OS-2410 for the Mandarin identification of the delisted entity.
