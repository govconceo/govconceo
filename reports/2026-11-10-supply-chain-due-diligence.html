Defense Supply-Chain Waivers Tighten on January 1, as FASCSA Orders Hit DeepSeek and Acronis

Key takeaways

  • Waiver cutoff. Executive Order 14415 of July 20, 2026, directs that on January 1, 2027, the Secretary of War and the military departments shall cease to issue 10 U.S.C. 4872(c)(1) waivers for covered materials, except where a formal mitigation plan is submitted and accepted by the Secretary or a designee.
  • Mapping mandate. By January 16, 2027 (180 days after the order), the Secretary must issue policy requiring all-tier primes and subcontractors to map critical supply chains, from raw materials to end-use products, for national-security-related Department of War acquisitions. Implementing regulations follow within 90 days after that policy is completed.
  • DeepSeek exclusion. DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 (released July 24, 2026; notice at 91 FR 66346, October 20, 2026) requires civilian executive branch agencies, other than national security systems, to exclude DeepSeek products and affiliates from procurements immediately and to remove covered products immediately or seek an exception.
  • Acronis removal clock. DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-02 (released July 31, 2026; notice at 91 FR 68528, October 30, 2026) requires immediate exclusion of Acronis covered articles and removal from agency information systems — including contractor-operated federal systems — by January 27, 2027, or an exception.
  • Buy American content. FAR Part 25, as published on Acquisition.gov, sets the Buy American domestic-content test at 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028 and 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029.
  • DHS PPE rule. DHS issued a final HSAR rule on Make PPE in America Act restrictions, effective October 19, 2026 (91 FR 59066).

Findings

Waivers end Jan 1 without a mitigation plan

On January 1, 2027, most new waivers under 10 U.S.C. 4872(c)(1) are to stop. Executive Order 14415, “Securing America’s Defense Supply Chains and Ensuring Domestic Acquisition of Critical Materials,” was signed July 20, 2026, and published in the Federal Register on July 23, 2026.

Section 2 of the order is the waiver restriction. A narrow path remains, and it is written into the order rather than left to informal practice.

Subsection (b) allows a waiver that would otherwise be prohibited if the prime contractor or subcontractor submits a formal mitigation plan, accepted by the Secretary or a designee. That plan must meet the order’s stated conditions:

  • Identify the source. The plan must identify the source of the covered material that would not be compliant with 10 U.S.C. 4872 absent a waiver.
  • Show exhaustive effort. It must document evidence of exhaustive efforts to acquire compliant covered material, or show that compliant material was not available when the non-compliant material was acquired.
  • Describe removal steps. It must describe the steps the prime or subcontractor will take to remove the non-compliant covered material from its supply chains.
  • Set a strict timeline. It must establish a strict projected timeline for complete implementation of the mitigation plan.

Waivers under 10 U.S.C. 4872(e) are also limited as provided in the order. That path includes an option that runs through a request to the Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs.

Mapping runs to raw materials; rules follow the policy

Section 3 sets a mapping mandate for all-tier defense suppliers on national-security-related Department of War acquisitions. The Secretary must issue the policy and implementation guidance within 180 days of the order, which is January 16, 2027.

The mandate covers prime contractors and subcontractors at any tier. It applies to Department of War acquisitions that support, implicate, or relate to United States national security, from raw materials to the end-use products those contractors deliver.

Implementing regulations are to be promulgated within 90 days after that policy is completed. Those regulations are to seek to avoid unduly burdening small businesses, non-traditional defense companies, and new entrants.

The proposed regulations are to require the following:

  • Indentured bill of materials. A complete indentured bill of materials tracing components, parts, equipment, software, and materials back to the origin of raw materials.
  • Supplier vetting. Written procedures to vet suppliers and subcontractors for financial risk; foreign ownership, control, or influence; and manufacturing and supply risk.
  • Unreliable-supplier bar. Subject to a stated exception, a prohibition on using covered material supplied by an “unreliable foreign supplier” as defined in the order.
  • 15-day notice. After vetting, contractors must implement and track mitigation and notify the Department of significant risks within 15 days.
  • 45-day plan. Contractors must submit a confidential corrective-action plan within 45 days and later submit a closeout report.

Section 4 is a separate directive with the same 180-day clock. Within 180 days, the Secretary is to initiate regulatory action to identify national-security-related acquisitions and to require contractors who rely on unreliable foreign suppliers in those supply chains to qualify and use alternative sources as soon as possible, consistent with law, safety, mission requirements, and existing contracts.

The order also directs the Secretary, within 90 days of the order, to develop a strategy to accelerate testing and qualification of new sources and materials. This report does not treat a public release of that 90-day strategy as confirmed, because no official strategy document was located.

FASCSA orders already bar DeepSeek and start an Acronis clock

Two Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act exclusion and removal orders are now in the official record. Both were signed by Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin and later published as Federal Register notices with the signed orders as official appendices.

Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 has an original release date of July 24, 2026. It applies to federal civilian executive branch agencies except for their national security systems and secure compartmented information systems. Required actions are:

  • Immediate exclusion. Exclude DeepSeek, DeepSeek-affiliated entities, and their products from civilian agency procurements, effective immediately after issuance, including from agency-approved products lists, service-provider equipment lists, e-commerce markets, and similar procurement functions.
  • Immediate removal. Remove products provided by DeepSeek and affiliates, and third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations, from civilian agency information technology systems immediately after issuance, or seek an exception.
  • Exception window. Exception requests must be submitted in writing within 60 days of issuance. The Secretary is to review the order within one year and annually thereafter.

DHS published notice of the DeepSeek order on October 20, 2026, at 91 FR 66346, including the signed order as an official appendix. That appendix is the text opened for these operational details.

Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-02 has an original release date of July 31, 2026. It directs civilian executive branch agencies to act as follows:

  • Immediate exclusion. Exclude Acronis AG and all affiliated entities, and any covered articles directly or indirectly supplied by them, from agency procurement actions immediately after issuance.
  • 180-day removal. Remove Acronis covered articles from agency information systems within 180 calendar days of issuance, or seek an exception. One hundred eighty calendar days after July 31, 2026, is January 27, 2027.
  • Exception window. Exception requests are likewise due in writing within 60 days.
  • Contractor-operated systems. A footnote in the signed order states that the order applies to federal information systems, including systems used or operated by an agency or by a contractor of an agency or other organization on behalf of an agency.

DHS published notice of the Acronis order on October 30, 2026, at 91 FR 68528.

Buy American rises to 75 percent in 2029; DHS PPE rules are already in effect

Domestic-content rules already in the FAR continue on a published schedule. FAR Part 25, as opened on Acquisition.gov, sets the test this way:

  • 65 percent. The domestic-content test for a domestic end product is 65 percent for items delivered in calendar years 2024 through 2028.
  • 75 percent. The test is 75 percent for items delivered starting in calendar year 2029, unless an alternate percentage is established for a contract in accordance with FAR 25.101(d).
  • Unknown origin. Components of unknown origin are treated as foreign.

DHS also issued a final Homeland Security Acquisition Regulation rule, “Make Personal Protective Equipment in America Act Restrictions on Foreign Acquisition” (HSAR Case 2024-003). It was published September 18, 2026, and is effective October 19, 2026 (91 FR 59066).

The rule amends HSAR parts 3025 and 3052 to codify how DHS complies with the Make PPE in America Act for specified types of personal protective equipment.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Defense bids that rely on a 10 U.S.C. 4872 waiver should treat January 1, 2027, as a hard change in official policy. New (c)(1) waivers are to cease except where an accepted mitigation plan is in place, as the order provides.

  • Source or plan. Offerors whose bills of materials include covered materials from sources that would need a waiver should plan either a compliant source or a mitigation plan the Department will accept.
  • No clause yet. The mapping and indentured-BOM requirements are not yet a published FAR or DFARS clause. They are a directive to the Secretary to issue policy by January 16, 2027, and regulations within 90 days after that policy is completed.
  • Solicitation previews. Solicitations may still begin to preview that direction, but the official contract language will be in the forthcoming policy and rules.

Compliance cost

Once the implementing regulations appear, the documentation and subcontract-management burden will be substantial, including for small businesses the order says the rules should not unduly burden.

  • Defense mapping. All-tier mapping, an indentured bill of materials back to raw materials, supplier vetting for financial, FOCI, and manufacturing risk, and the 15-day / 45-day notice and corrective-action clocks will drive that cost.
  • Civilian FASCSA. On the civilian side, FASCSA orders already have immediate procurement consequences for DeepSeek and Acronis products. The Acronis order’s 180-day removal clock includes contractor-operated federal information systems.
  • Content thresholds. Buy American content at 65 percent is already the rule for deliveries through 2028. The 75 percent test applies to deliveries starting in 2029 and should be priced into multiyear production now.
  • DHS PPE. DHS PPE purchases are under the new HSAR restrictions as of October 19, 2026.

Execution

Companies supplying civilian agencies should treat the DeepSeek and Acronis orders as already in force. The agency exception windows (60 days from each order’s issuance) have already closed.

  • Civilian systems. Confirm that DeepSeek models, DeepSeek-derived third-party tools, and Acronis covered articles are out of agency-facing offerings and, for Acronis, off contractor-operated federal systems by January 27, 2027, unless an official exception applies.
  • Defense inventory. Defense suppliers should inventory 4872-covered materials, identify any current waivers, and prepare mitigation plans or alternate sources before January 1, 2027.
  • Be ready to map. They should also be ready to produce indentured bills of materials when the Section 3 policy is issued.

Watch next

  • Jan 1, 2027. January 1, 2027 is the date in EO 14415 on which most new 10 U.S.C. 4872(c)(1) waivers are to cease, except as the order provides.
  • Jan 16, 2027. January 16, 2027 is 180 days after EO 14415, by which the Secretary is to develop the all-tier mapping policy and implementation guidance, and to initiate the Section 4 regulatory action on alternative sources.
  • Jan 27, 2027. January 27, 2027 is 180 calendar days after the July 31, 2026, Acronis exclusion and removal order, by which covered articles must be removed from covered agency information systems, or an exception obtained.
  • Calendar year 2029. FAR Part 25 raises the Buy American domestic-content test to 75 percent for items delivered starting in that year.
  • Regulations after the policy. Implementing regulations under EO 14415 Section 3 are due 90 days after the mapping policy is completed; that follow-on date is not fixed until the policy itself is issued.
