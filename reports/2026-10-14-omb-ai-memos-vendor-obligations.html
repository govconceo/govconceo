Three OMB Memos Set the Terms for Selling AI to Civilian Agencies. Here Is What Vendors Must Deliver

Key takeaways

  • Three memos govern. OMB M-25-21 (use and risk management) and M-25-22 (acquisition), both dated April 3, 2025, and M-26-04 (Unbiased AI Principles for LLMs), dated December 11, 2025, set the federal civilian baseline for AI contracts. All three are listed on OMB’s memoranda page as of October 14, 2026.
  • Already in contracts. M-25-22 applies to contracts awarded under solicitations issued on or after the date 180 days after April 3, 2025, and to options exercised after that date.
  • No training on agency data. Agencies must ensure contracts permanently prohibit using non-public agency inputs and outputs to further train publicly or commercially available AI, absent explicit agency consent.
  • Testing access is a term, not a favor. Vendors must give agencies the access and time needed for independent evaluation. Contracts must not bar agencies from internally disclosing how vendors test or what the results were.
  • LLM documentation is a minimum. Under M-26-04, LLM solicitations must request an acceptable use policy, model, system or data cards, end-user resources and a feedback mechanism. Agencies should make these requirements material to payment.
  • Incidental use is out of scope. AI that a contractor uses at its own option, when not required to fulfil the contract, is excluded from M-25-22 and M-26-04.

Findings

Two memos set the rules, and a third adds LLM terms

The obligations that reach vendors come mostly from M-25-22, “Driving Efficient Acquisition of Artificial Intelligence in Government.” It tells agencies which terms to put in solicitations and contracts. M-25-21 sets the agency’s own risk-management duties, which vendors must help meet.

M-26-04 layers on requirements for large language models, implementing Executive Order 14319. Its scope is any LLM an agency procures, not national security systems. It also asks agencies to modify existing LLM contracts, to the extent practicable, before options are exercised.

  • M-25-21. Governance, the definition of “high-impact AI,” and minimum practices such as pre-deployment testing, an AI impact assessment, ongoing monitoring and human oversight.
  • M-25-22. Acquisition terms: IP and government data, privacy, vendor lock-in, testing, performance and new-feature notification.
  • M-26-04. LLM transparency deliverables and the two Unbiased AI Principles: truth-seeking and ideological neutrality.

What is out of scope matters as much as what is in

M-25-22 excludes common commercial products with embedded AI, such as a word processor, and AI used incidentally by a contractor during performance. It also does not apply to national security systems or to elements of the Intelligence Community.

M-26-04 likewise does not cover a contractor’s incidental use of AI for its own administrative tasks. Agencies are not required to apply it to LLMs acquired under a free, open-source license, though they should do due diligence on those models before use.

  • Practical test. If the government is buying the AI, or relying on it to deliver the work, expect these terms. If your team uses an AI tool on its own initiative, the memos do not require these terms, though an agency may still ask for disclosure.

Agency data cannot train your commercial models

The data provision is the term vendors will notice first. It is drafted as a permanent prohibition, not a default that a vendor can negotiate away in its terms of service.

M-25-21 points the same way. It tells agencies to protect government data from being used to train or improve a vendor’s commercial offerings without the agency’s express permission, and to retain sufficient rights to federal government data and any improvements to that data.

Contract terms the memo lists by name

M-25-22 directs agencies to include specific categories of terms in AI contracts. The categories below are taken from its contract-terms section.

  • IP and government data. Terms must match each agency’s standard approach to data ownership and IP, which agencies were to set within 200 days.
  • Vendor lock-in. Protections can include knowledge transfer, data and model portability, rights to code and models produced in performance, and transparency in licensing and pricing.
  • M-25-21 compliance. For high-impact uses, contracts must ensure compliance with the minimum risk-management practices.
  • Testing. Vendors must provide access and time for independent evaluation. Contracts must detail the vendor’s testing and validation procedures.
  • Performance. Agencies are encouraged to require monitoring, performance standards before new versions deploy, and roll-back if a new version falls short.
  • New features. Agencies should consider requiring notice before new AI features or components are integrated into delivered systems.

High-impact uses bring disclosure and documentation duties

When practicable, agencies must say in a solicitation whether a planned use is high-impact, or is reasonably likely to become so. They must also tell vendors what documentation they will need to complete the M-25-21 AI impact assessment.

M-25-21 lists the assessment’s contents. They include the intended purpose and expected benefit, the quality and appropriateness of the data used, the potential impacts, and plans for reassessment. Vendors whose systems fall in this tier should expect to supply that information.

  • Agency deadline passed. M-25-21 gave agencies 365 days, to April 3, 2026, to document the minimum practices for high-impact AI or stop using non-compliant systems, subject to waivers by the agency’s Chief AI Officer.

LLM vendors owe four documents, and maybe more

M-26-04 requires that solicitations for LLMs request a minimum set of transparency materials. It notes that it is uncommon for a vendor to produce all three types of card.

  • Acceptable use policy. Typically drafted by the original LLM developer.
  • Model, system or data cards. Summaries of training, identified risks and mitigations, and benchmark scores.
  • End-user resources. Tutorials, developer guides and similar tools.
  • Feedback mechanism. A route, such as an inbox or product contact, to report outputs that violate the Unbiased AI Principles.

Agencies may ask for more, such as pre- and post-training actions, system prompts, red-teaming results or third-party modifications. The memo tells agencies to avoid compelling disclosure of sensitive technical data, such as specific model weights.

GSA has already turned the memos into a clause

For GSA-awarded vehicles, these policies now appear as contract text in GSAR 552.239-7001, issued through GSA’s RFO deviation guidance. That clause restates the training ban, adds deletion and portability duties, and sets notice clocks. Our line-by-line report covers it.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Read every AI solicitation for the M-25-22 signals: a high-impact flag, lock-in language and evaluation of portability. M-25-22 tells agencies to signal interest in proposals that reduce lock-in, so portability is a likely discriminator.

  • Price the testing. Independent evaluation access and testing support are contract obligations. Build them into the price, not an assumption of goodwill.
  • Resellers need the developer. M-26-04 documents come from the model developer. Secure them before you bid if you resell or integrate someone else’s model.

Compliance cost

The new cost is mostly documentation and engineering for data isolation. Standard commercial terms that allow training on customer data will not survive these contracts.

  • Data segregation. Show that agency inputs and outputs are excluded from training pipelines, for every model in the stack.
  • Change control. Notice of new features and version roll-back require release processes that can treat a federal tenant differently.

Execution

M-26-04 makes its requirements material to payment and supports default termination for vendors that refuse corrective action. Treat noncompliance findings as a contract-risk event, not a support ticket.

  • Option years. Existing LLM contracts may be modified before options are exercised. Expect M-26-04 terms to arrive at option time.
  • Exit readiness. M-25-22’s closeout section expects agencies to implement data and derived-product transfer terms when a contract will not be extended. Plan the export path now.

Watch next

  • Agency AI acquisition policies. M-25-22 required each agency to set a standard process for data ownership and IP in AI procurements. Watch for agency-level clauses that differ from GSA’s.
  • Dec 11, 2027. M-26-04 is scheduled to sunset two years after issuance unless extended.
  • FAR overhaul. No FAR text implementing these memos was found in the current FAR parts or in the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul proposed rules reviewed for this report.
