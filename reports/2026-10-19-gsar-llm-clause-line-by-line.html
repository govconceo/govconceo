GSA’s LLM Clause, Line by Line: What GSAR 552.239-7001 Requires and Why It Overrides Your Terms of Service

Key takeaways

  • It beats your terms of service. The clause is incorporated into the “schedule of supplies/services,” the top of the commercial order of precedence. If it conflicts with a vendor’s terms of service, click-wrap or licence, the clause takes precedence.
  • Narrow trigger, self-deleting. It applies when GSA buys a system where LLM functionality is a material feature and Government Data goes in or comes out. It imposes no obligation for a contractor’s internal back-office AI or for incidental AI features the Government does not require, access or feed data to.
  • Effective dates. RGO-2026-01’s Attachment A lists GSAR Part 539, which tells contracting officers when to use the clause, as effective March 16, 2026. It lists Part 552, which holds the clause text, as effective October 19, 2026.
  • Seven clocks to track. The clause sets notice periods of 72 hours, 5 business days, 7 calendar days, 30 days and 120 days. It also requires logs to be kept for 90 days.
  • Capped exit liability. If GSA terminates for cause after written notice and a failure to fix, the contractor owes reasonable decommissioning costs. These are capped at 25% of the affected order and exclude re-procurement costs.

Findings

When the clause applies, and when it deletes itself

GSAR 539.71(a) tells contracting officers to insert the clause when buying an “LLM, generative assistant, chatbot, agentic system, LLM-enabled document or productivity tool, or similar system.” Two conditions must both hold: LLM functionality is a material feature, and Government Data will go into or come out of the LLM.

Paragraph (a)(1)(ii) makes the clause self-deleting in two cases, unless the contracting officer says otherwise. Both cases are drawn narrowly.

  • Internal tools. The contractor’s internal business, back-office or performance-support systems that the Government does not receive or access, even if they incidentally process Government Data.
  • Incidental features. LLM functionality that is incidental to a commercial product whose main purpose is not AI. This applies only if the Government does not require it, access it or use it to process Government Data.

Under GSAR 501.107(d), changes apply to solicitations issued on or after their effective date. Contracting officers may use them in earlier solicitations if award comes on or after the effective date. They may also add them to existing contracts with appropriate consideration.

Who it flows down to

The clause must flow to subcontractors doing AI design, development, deployment, or operation and monitoring, as described in Appendix A of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. It flows only where they handle Government Data in connection with the LLM.

Paragraphs on origin and foreign control, including (f)(2), (f)(3) and (i)(1)(iii), need not flow to Open-LLM Components or Fully Open Models. The definition of “LLM” is broad. It covers the whole system around the model, including hosting, system prompts, retrieval, APIs, guardrails, monitoring and workflows.

Paragraph (c): the clause outranks commercial terms

This is the paragraph that changes how terms of service work on GSA orders. It places the clause in the “schedule of supplies/services,” which is first in the order of precedence in FAR 52.212-4(s).

The clause adds that nothing in it overrides applicable law, regulation or mandatory government clauses. Existing commercial-terms rules still apply alongside it.

  • FAR 52.212-4(u). Terms of service clauses requiring the Government to indemnify a vendor in ways that would violate the Anti-Deficiency Act are unenforceable. Clicking “I agree” does not bind the Government to them.
  • GSAR 552.212-4(v) in the deviation. It removes from commercial supplier agreements any terms applying state or foreign law, forum selection and automatic renewal. A vendor’s unilateral changes to the agreement after award that are material and inconsistent with the contract are unenforceable against the Government.

Paragraph (d): due diligence without new paperwork

The prime must use best efforts to vet subcontractors in the AI roles, and notify the contracting officer within 72 hours of actual knowledge of a “material violation.” The clause defines that as a violation with a significant adverse effect on performance, government rights, security, confidentiality or compliance.

Compliance can be shown with existing evidence. The clause says contractors need not create duplicative documentation.

  • Accepted evidence. Flowdown; subcontractor attestation backed by independently verifiable evidence where practicable; public information for open components; and existing artifacts such as model cards, FedRAMP packages, SOC 2 reports or ISO/IEC 27001 certifications.
  • Shared responsibility. Where compliance depends on how the Government configures the LLM, the contractor must document that and give the Government the means to verify it.

Paragraph (e): ownership, licence and handling

The Government owns Government Data, meaning inputs and outputs, and Custom Developments. The contractor keeps the underlying LLM, base models and Background Data, and the clause says nothing transfers ownership of the contractor’s pre-existing materials.

The handling rules apply unless the contracting officer authorizes otherwise. Several have direct engineering consequences.

  • Prohibited uses. No training or fine-tuning on Government Data, no use for marketing or business decisions, no retention beyond scope, and no selling or licensing.
  • Access. Access to Government Data is limited to authorized personnel. Zero-persistence architectures that do not retain Government Data satisfy the access-control requirements.
  • Location and segregation. Data stays on agreed premises or FedRAMP-authorized services. FedRAMP compliance at the specified level is deemed to satisfy logical segregation.
  • Deletion. At the end, delete Government Data and Custom Developments, including fine-tuned weights, embeddings, indexes and caches, and document it in writing.

Paragraph (f): disclosures, origin and incidents

Paragraph (f) holds most of the reporting duties. Several can be met with existing model cards, FedRAMP packages, SOC reports or ISO certifications if they are current.

  • (f)(1) Disclose the LLMs. List the LLMs used to process Government Data, by the contract date or within 120 days of starting work.
  • (f)(2) US models preferred. Maximize use of LLMs developed and operated in the US. Core components may not be run by entities under the control of adversary foreign governments, as defined in 15 CFR 791.4.
  • (f)(3) Foreign modifications. Disclose any modification made at a non-US government’s request, or to comply with non-US law, within 30 days of award or of becoming aware.
  • (f)(4) Traceability. Where the LLM uses reasoning, retrieval or agent steps, provide a summary of the recorded steps through the interface, a documented API and an audit trail. The clause says this is “not a faithful explanation of the model’s internal reasoning.”
  • (f)(5) Incidents. Notify within 72 hours, then give daily updates. Keep logs for 90 days and file the CISA incident form. FedRAMP reports that contain substantially the same information can satisfy overlapping duties.

Paragraphs (g) to (i): privacy, portability and change control

Paragraph (g) requires contractors to make available any commercial tools that let the Government block or remove PII. It also says the Government will not disclose a contractor’s confidential documentation absent a legal requirement.

Paragraph (h) requires export of Government Data in open, machine-readable formats with documented APIs and migration tooling. It forbids proprietary formats or fees that materially impair migration.

  • (i)(1) Advance notice. Give 30 calendar days’ written notice before changing LLMs, developers or operators, or reducing data protections. The same applies to a change in FedRAMP status or a change made to comply with non-US government rules.
  • (i)(2) Deprecation. Give at least 30 days’ concurrent access to a successor model for major versions and 15 days for minor ones. If a third-party provider deprecates the model, notify within five business days.
  • (i)(3) Degradation. Notify within seven calendar days of a change that increases bias, weakens guardrails or degrades truthfulness.

Paragraph (j): evaluation, suspension and exit costs

The contractor must use reasonable efforts to prioritize accuracy and objectivity and to acknowledge uncertainty. The Government may run its own benchmarks on the deployed LLM at any time and keep those benchmarks confidential, unless they are the basis for an adverse action.

The Government may suspend use of the LLM at any time. Decommissioning-cost liability arises only after a termination for cause that follows written notice and a failure to fix.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Decide early whether the clause applies to you. If AI is incidental to your product and the Government will not use it or feed it data, the exception may apply. Say so in the proposal, so the contracting officer can confirm it.

  • Ship the artifacts. Current model cards, FedRAMP packages and SOC 2 reports can satisfy disclosure and due-diligence duties. Have them ready for evaluation.
  • Know your model’s origin. Paragraph (f)(2) prefers US-developed models. Be able to document who develops, hosts and controls each model in your stack.

Compliance cost

The heaviest costs are change control and traceability. Model upgrades now need notice, concurrent access and documentation for each GSA order.

  • Federal release track. You may need a separate release schedule for federal tenants to meet the 30-day notice and concurrent-access rules.
  • Audit trail. Agentic and retrieval features need a step summary through an API, not just the user interface.

Execution

Rewrite commercial terms for GSA orders rather than hoping the precedence rule fixes conflicts. Terms allowing training on customer content, unilateral changes or automatic renewals conflict with the clause or the commercial-terms rules.

  • Upstream contracts. If you resell a third-party model, your contract with that provider must let you meet the 5-business-day deprecation notice, the 72-hour incident notice and the (f)(3) foreign-modification disclosure.
  • Deletion evidence. Build a documented process to delete fine-tuned weights, embeddings, indexes and caches at contract end.

Watch next

  • Formal GSAR rulemaking. RGO-2026-01 says formal rulemaking, with notice and comment, will follow the deviations.
  • Updated deviation PDF. GSA says it will keep updating the deviation PDF on Acquisition.gov as new parts take effect, with a public feedback form.
  • Agency adoption outside GSA. Other agencies follow their own M-25-22 processes. Watch for similar clauses in agency supplements.
