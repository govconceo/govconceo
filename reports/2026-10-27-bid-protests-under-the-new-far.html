Bid Protests Under the New FAR: What Changes at the Agency, What Stays at GAO and the Court of Federal Claims

Key takeaways

  • Part 33 gets a purpose statement. Proposed FAR 33.100 says protests are not a substitute for debriefings. It says incumbents "should not use protests as a way to disrupt transition or induce contract extensions" without sufficient grounds.
  • Contracting officers would document incumbent protests. Proposed 33.103(e) calls for a finding when an incumbent's protest disrupts transition or induces an extension.
  • Agency protests get more disclosure. On independent review, a protester could get redacted copies of its final technical evaluation and the source selection decision, and add grounds.
  • GAO rules are untouched. The FAR would point to 4 CFR part 21 instead of restating it. The 10-day filing rules, the 100-day decision deadline and the statutory stay come from GAO's rules and 31 U.S.C. 3553, not the FAR.
  • COFC is largely outside Part 33. Proposed 33.101(b) says the subpart, except 33.100 and 33.103(c), does not apply where the Court of Federal Claims has jurisdiction.
  • GAO's FY2025 numbers. 1,688 cases filed (down 6%), a 14% sustain rate and a 52% effectiveness rate.

Findings

The new purpose statement sets the tone

Proposed FAR 33.100 lists five objectives for the protest system. They include deciding protests "without undue delay," correcting errors quickly, and "deter[ring] and discourag[ing] abuse" by "requiring clear and substantiated allegations."

Proposed 33.100(d) adds that the protest process "is not intended to serve as a way for offerors to get post-award explanations, or debriefings."

What changes for agency-level protests

Proposed 33.104-4 would make three changes:

  • Reporting up. Contracting officers would report protests to the head of the contracting activity (33.104-4(a)(4)(ii)).
  • More disclosure. A protester that elects independent review above the contracting officer could get a redacted copy of the agency's final technical evaluation of its proposal and of the source selection decision (33.104-4(a)(5)(ii)(B)). The preamble says this applies to any type of procurement, including Parts 8, 12 and 15.
  • New grounds. The protester could raise additional grounds within a reasonable time set by the reviewing official (33.104-4(a)(5)(ii)(C)).

The preamble says the goal is to "build confidence in agency protests, and resolve more protests at the agency level."

What changes for incumbents

Proposed 33.103(e) says the contracting officer "should document a finding when a protest is filed by an incumbent contractor that disrupts transition or induces an extension of their current contract." It cross-references 33.100(e).

The proposal does not state a penalty or consequence for such a finding in the text opened. It is a documentation requirement.

GAO: the FAR stops restating GAO's rules

The preamble says the rule would "remove regulatory text that repeats or summarizes" GAO's regulations and instead point to 4 CFR part 21. GAO's own rules remain the source:

  • Solicitation defects. Must be protested before bid opening or the closing time for initial proposals (4 CFR 21.2(a)(1)).
  • Other grounds. Must be filed within 10 days after the basis is known or should have been known (21.2(a)(2)).
  • Debriefing exception. Where a debriefing is requested and required, the protest may not be filed before the debriefing date offered and is due within 10 days after the debriefing is held.
  • Decision deadline. 100 days from filing, or 65 days under the express option (21.9).

The stay comes from statute, not the FAR

Under 31 U.S.C. 3553(d), if the agency gets notice of a protest within the statutory window, the contracting officer may not authorize performance, or must direct the contractor to stop work.

The window ends on the later of 10 days after award or 5 days after a required debriefing date offered. For DoD, the 5-day period does not start until written responses to debriefing questions are delivered (3553(d)(4)(B)).

Task orders: the thresholds are statutory

Protests of task and delivery orders are limited by statute:

  • Title 10 agencies, including DoD. Allowed only for orders over $35,000,000, or on scope, period or maximum-value grounds (10 U.S.C. 3406(f)).
  • Civilian agencies. Allowed only for orders over $10,000,000, or on the same grounds (41 U.S.C. 4106(f)).
  • Forum. GAO has exclusive jurisdiction over the value-based order protests.

GAO reported that 359 cases closed in FY2025 arose under its task-order jurisdiction.

Missing the stay window has real costs

GAO's FY2025 report describes ATP Gov, LLC (B-422938, Dec. 12, 2024). The protester did not file within five days of its debriefing, so there was no automatic stay, and the awardee kept performing a delivery order.

GAO sustained the protest. The Air Force then declined to reevaluate or resolicit, citing cost, delay and national security, and agreed to pay only protest costs. GAO recommended that Congress pass a private bill to reimburse proposal preparation costs.

GAO also noted the protester could not go to the Court of Federal Claims because the protest concerned a delivery order (10 U.S.C. 3406(f)).

The Court of Federal Claims sits outside most of Part 33

Proposed 33.101(b) says the subpart, except 33.100 and 33.103(c), does not apply where the Court of Federal Claims has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. 1491. Proposed 33.106 points to the court's own rules.

Proposed 33.103(c) keeps one cross-forum rule. Where funds would otherwise lapse, they stay available for 100 days after a final ruling on a protest at GAO, the court or the agency (31 U.S.C. 1558).

GAO bases its effectiveness rate on a protester "obtaining some form of relief from the agency," through voluntary corrective action or a sustain. GAO says it issued final decisions within 100 days for all protests filed in FY2025. The FY2025 filings included 1,617 protests, 24 cost claims and 47 requests for reconsideration.

GAO listed the most common sustain grounds as unreasonable technical evaluation, unreasonable cost or price evaluation, and unreasonable rejection of a proposal. Alternative dispute resolution was used in 53 cases, with a 91% success rate.

The impact estimate

The rule's regulatory impact discussion says the Part 33 changes are "not expected to have a significant impact on contractors or subcontracts." It notes added agency burden from tracking protests reported to the head of the contracting activity.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Debrief first. Proposed 33.100(d) frames protests as no substitute for debriefings. Use the debriefing to test grounds.
  • Weigh the agency forum. Redacted evaluation and source selection documents, plus the right to add grounds, make an agency protest more informative than it is today.
  • Incumbents: build the record. A finding under proposed 33.103(e) would sit in the contract file. Protest only on grounds you can substantiate.

Compliance cost

  • The deadlines do not move. GAO's 10-day rules and the statutory stay window are unchanged by the FAR proposal.
  • Expect agency tracking. Protests reported to the head of the contracting activity create agency-level data on who protests.

Execution

  • Calendar the debriefing. The stay window and GAO's deadline both key off the debriefing date offered, or for DoD the written answers.
  • Check order values. The $35 million and $10 million thresholds decide whether an order protest is possible.
  • Plan for transition. Incumbent protests that delay transition will be documented if the rule is finalized.

Watch next

  • Final rule for FAR Case 2026-001. Not published as of Oct 27, 2026.
  • GAO FY2026 report. GAO's FY2025 report was dated Dec 12, 2025. A FY2026 report would be expected on a similar schedule.
  • Model Part 33 deviation. It was issued Aug 21, 2025 and may already be in use at agencies.
