GSA’s LLM Clause Sets Data-Use Rules; OMB Rescinds a Software Attestation Mandate and Sets 2030 Cryptography Targets

Key takeaways

  • GSA LLM clause. GSA class deviation RGO-2026-01, signed January 15, 2026, includes GSAR 552.239-7001 (GSAR Deviation) (Sept 2026). The clause applies when the Government is procuring an LLM or similar system in which LLM functionality is a material feature and Government Data will be submitted directly to, or produced by, the LLM, with stated exceptions for qualifying internal tools and incidental or ancillary use.
  • Core clause duties. The official clause text requires notice to the contracting officer within 72 hours after the contractor obtains actual knowledge of a material violation; prohibits using Government Data to train, fine-tune, or otherwise improve an LLM; retains Government ownership of Government Data and custom developments; and caps decommissioning-cost liability at 25 percent of the value of the affected task or delivery order when the Government terminates for cause after written notice and a failure to remediate.
  • Refresh 34 plan. Official GSA Refresh 34 Q&A restates when 552.239-7001 applies and states that GSA plans to issue Refresh 34 on November 12, 2026. The opened Q&A does not state a 90-day acceptance window.
  • Software attestation mandate rescinded. OMB Memorandum M-26-05, January 23, 2026, rescinds M-22-18 and M-23-16. Agencies may choose to use the governmentwide Secure Software Development Attestation Form; they are no longer under that prior governmentwide mandate. They may also require a software bill of materials upon request.
  • Unbiased AI clause content. OMB Memorandum M-26-04 directs agencies to include contractual requirements addressing the Unbiased AI Principles in LLM solicitations and orders issued after the memorandum, and to update procurement policies no later than March 11, 2026. The memorandum sunsets two years after issuance unless OMB provides otherwise.
  • Post-quantum FAR proposal due Dec 19. Executive Order 14412 of June 22, 2026, directs the FAR Council, within 180 days of the order (December 19, 2026), to publish a proposed FAR rule requiring covered contractors to comply by December 31, 2030, with NIST FIPS, including FIPS that incorporate post-quantum cryptography algorithms. OMB Memorandum M-26-15 sets a December 31, 2030 objective for agencies’ prioritized migration of cryptographic systems they own or operate, and requires TLS 1.3 or a successor no later than January 2, 2030. That memorandum does not apply to national security systems.

Findings

GSA’s LLM clause applies when the model is a material feature and handles Government Data

GSA’s Senior Procurement Executive issued class deviation RGO-2026-01 on January 15, 2026. The official compiled text opened for this report includes clause GSAR 552.239-7001 (GSAR Deviation) (Sept 2026), Basic Safeguarding of Large Language Model systems.

Paragraph (a) of the clause, and GSA’s official Refresh 34 Q&A, state that the clause applies only to the extent the Government is procuring an LLM or similar system where LLM functionality is a material feature and Government Data will be submitted directly to or produced by the LLM, with exceptions for qualifying internal contractor tools and incidental or ancillary functionality.

The official RGO PDF opened for this report is dated January 15, 2026, and the clause is marked “(Sept 2026).” This report does not state a separate October 19, 2026 effective date, because that date was not found in the official documents opened.

Where the clause applies, these duties follow:

  • Order of precedence. The clause takes precedence over conflicting contractor license terms for purposes of the order-of-precedence provision in FAR 52.212-4 or GSAR 552.212-4, as applicable.
  • 72-hour notice. The contractor must notify the contracting officer within 72 hours after obtaining actual knowledge of a material violation.
  • Subcontractor diligence. The contractor must use best efforts to conduct due diligence when selecting and overseeing subcontractors engaged in AI design, development, deployment, or operation and monitoring tasks described in NIST AI RMF 1.0, Appendix A.
  • Government ownership. The Government retains ownership of Government Data and custom developments. The contractor receives only a limited, revocable license to use that data for performance, specified security telemetry, technical support, and other uses the contracting officer expressly authorizes.
  • No training on Government Data. Training, fine-tuning, or otherwise improving an LLM — including a third-party model — with Government Data is a prohibited use, as is using that data for advertising, marketing, or other customers.
  • Location and design. Government Data generally must remain in agreed premises or FedRAMP-authorized services. The contractor must use reasonable efforts to design, train, and configure the LLM to prioritize accuracy, scientific inquiry, and objectivity, and to acknowledge uncertainty where information is incomplete or contradictory.
  • Decommissioning cap. If the contracting officer terminates for cause for failure to remediate after specific written notice, the contractor is liable for reasonable decommissioning costs, not to exceed 25 percent of the value of the affected task or delivery order, and not including re-procurement or replacement-system development costs.
  • Successor-model overlap. Before discontinuing a model, the contractor must use reasonable efforts to provide concurrent access to a successor model for at least 30 calendar days for a major version and 15 calendar days for a minor version.

Due diligence may be shown through flowdown, attestation supported where practicable by independently verifiable evidence, identification of openly accessible information for open-LLM components, best-effort documentation for open-weight models, or existing artifacts such as FedRAMP packages, SOC 2 reports, or ISO/IEC 27001 materials.

GSA’s official Refresh 34 Q&A, opened as a GSA PDF, answers a question about 552.239-7001 and, separately, states: “We plan to issue on November 12, 2026,” in response to a question about Refresh 34 being delayed a week beyond November 6. The opened Q&A does not state a 90-day mass-modification acceptance window.

OMB rescinded the governmentwide software-attestation mandate and added Unbiased AI requirements

On the software-assurance side, OMB Memorandum M-26-05, “Adopting a Risk-based Approach to Software and Hardware Security,” January 23, 2026, rescinds M-22-18 and companion memorandum M-23-16. The governmentwide attestation mandate in the rescinded memoranda is no longer in force.

Agencies remain responsible for the security of software and hardware on their networks and must maintain a complete inventory and risk-based assurance policies. They may choose to use the governmentwide Secure Software Development Attestation Form developed under the rescinded policy, and they may adopt contractual terms requiring a current software bill of materials upon request.

OMB Memorandum M-26-04, “Increasing Public Trust in Artificial Intelligence Through Unbiased AI Principles,” implements Executive Order 14319. It requires agencies to include contractual requirements addressing the Unbiased AI Principles (truth-seeking and ideological neutrality) in any solicitation or order for procurement of an LLM issued after the date of the memorandum, and to update procurement policies no later than March 11, 2026.

Incidental contractor use of AI for the contractor’s own administration, when not directed or necessary to fulfill requirements, is outside the memorandum’s scope. The memorandum ceases to have force two years after issuance unless the OMB Director provides otherwise.

A FAR post-quantum proposal is directed by December 19, 2026, with a 2030 contractor date

Cryptographic migration now has contractor-facing rulemaking on a dated calendar. Executive Order 14412 of June 22, 2026, “Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks,” sets that timetable. One hundred eighty days after June 22, 2026, is December 19, 2026.

OMB Memorandum M-26-15, “Execution of the Migration to Post-Quantum Cryptography,” implements the same effort for agency-owned or agency-operated systems. Agencies must execute a prioritized migration with the objective of mitigating as much quantum risk as feasible by December 31, 2030, and must support TLS 1.3 or a successor as soon as practicable, but not later than January 2, 2030.

The memorandum does not apply to national security systems. It points agencies to CISA’s publication on product categories for technologies that use post-quantum cryptography standards when considering vendor and third-party software.

GAO report GAO-26-107859, April 2026, opened from an archived copy of the official gao.gov PDF, found that selected agencies were not prepared to share knowledge about AI acquisitions through GSA’s web-based repository because their policies did not require officials to collect lessons learned. GAO made four recommendations: that DoD, DHS, GSA, and VA update their policies to require officials to systematically collect lessons learned related to AI acquisitions.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

For GSA and orders that incorporate 552.239-7001, offerors should decide at proposal time whether LLM functionality is a material feature and whether Government Data will be submitted to or produced by the model.

  • If the clause applies. The bid must accept Government ownership of Government Data and custom developments, a ban on training or fine-tuning with that data, 72-hour material-violation notice, flowdown or alternative diligence for covered subcontractors, and the 25 percent decommissioning-cost cap on a for-cause termination after notice.
  • License terms yield. License terms that claim broader data rights will yield to the clause.
  • Exceptions are narrow. Incidental or purely internal tools may fall within the stated exceptions, but the official Q&A sends product-specific questions to the ordering activity.

Compliance cost

Where the clause applies, the new costs are operational, not theoretical. Other OMB memos change the software-assurance and Unbiased AI floor around it.

  • LLM clause costs. These include data-segregation and localization in FedRAMP-authorized or agreed environments, audit logging that does not expose Government Data, model-deprecation overlap windows, and diligence artifacts for subcontractors on NIST AI RMF task categories.
  • M-26-05. The memorandum lowers the governmentwide floor on software attestations — the prior mandate is rescinded — but an agency can still demand the form or an SBOM by contract.
  • M-26-04. The memorandum adds Unbiased AI clause content for new LLM buys.
  • Post-quantum. The coming FAR PQC proposal, if published as EO 14412 directs, would create a December 31, 2030 compliance date for covered contractors on NIST FIPS, including post-quantum algorithms, which is a longer-cycle engineering cost, not a 2026 clause by itself.

Execution

Contractors already delivering LLMs that process Government Data should map current practices against the official clause: no training on Government Data, 72-hour notice procedures, and written responsibility for configurations the Government controls.

  • Watch Refresh 34. Watch GSA’s planned November 12, 2026, Refresh 34 issuance for any mass-modification language that accompanies the official Q&A discussion of 552.239-7001.
  • Watch the FAR PQC proposal. Track Federal Register publication of the FAR PQC proposed rule against the December 19, 2026, deadline in EO 14412.
