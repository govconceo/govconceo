Who Owns the Data, the Model and the Outputs? How Federal Rules Split Rights in AI Contracts

Key takeaways

  • Government data stays government. OMB tells agencies to retain rights to federal data and to improvements to it. GSA’s LLM clause states that the Government owns all Government Data, meaning both inputs and outputs, and all Custom Developments.
  • Your model stays yours. Under GSAR 552.239-7001, the contractor keeps ownership of the underlying LLM, base models and Background Data. The Government gets a royalty-free licence to use the LLM, limited to the duration and scope of the work.
  • Fine-tunes belong to the job. Custom Developments include changes made through training or fine-tuning for the Government. At the end of the contract, fine-tuned weights, embeddings, indexes and caches must be deleted unless the contracting officer directs otherwise.
  • No training on agency data. OMB M-25-22 requires contracts to permanently prohibit using non-public agency inputs and outputs to further train commercial AI without explicit agency consent. The GSA clause lists training and fine-tuning as prohibited uses.
  • The FAR itself is silent on AI. FAR 12.212 still says commercial software is bought under licences customarily provided to the public. A September 18, 2026 proposed rule would replace FAR Subpart 27.4 with DFARS-style data-rights rules; comments closed October 19, 2026.

Findings

OMB set the policy: keep the data, protect it from training

The data-rights rules for federal AI start in two OMB memos dated April 3, 2025. They are written as instructions to agencies, but agencies carry them into contract terms.

  • M-25-21. Agencies should ensure contracts retain sufficient rights to federal government data and any improvements to that data. Contract terms should protect it from being used to train or improve a vendor’s commercial offerings without express agency permission.
  • M-25-22. Each agency had 200 days to set a standard process for data ownership and IP rights in AI procurements. Contract terms on IP and data must follow that process.

M-25-22 also names the rights that prevent lock-in. Its list includes “providing agencies with rights to code and models produced in performance of a contract,” alongside knowledge transfer and data and model portability.

GSA turned the policy into ownership language

GSAR 552.239-7001, issued in GSA’s RFO deviation guidance RGO-2026-01, is the most detailed federal contract text on AI data rights found for this report. It applies when GSA buys an LLM or similar system where LLM functionality is a material feature and Government Data goes into or comes out of it.

The clause starts with definitions, and they do most of the work. “Government Data” means Data Inputs plus Data Outputs.

  • Data Inputs. Everything submitted to the LLM by or for the Government, including prompts, system prompts, source data, documents and knowledge bases. Background Data and pre-existing contractor IP are excluded.
  • Data Outputs. Content the LLM generates in response, and changes made to inputs. The definition names derivative data, synthetic data and anonymized data. It excludes system-level data such as performance metrics, token counts, metadata and logs that contain no Government Data or Government Usage Context.
  • Background Data. The contractor’s proprietary content and IP that existed before the contract or was developed independently. This includes material pulled in through retrieval, vector stores, embeddings or tool calls.

Model weights split two ways

The clause separates the vendor’s base model from what is built for the Government. The contractor “retains ownership of the underlying LLM, base models, and Background Data,” under paragraph (e)(1)(vi).

Custom Development, by contrast, covers modifications, configurations and enhancements developed specifically for the Government. That expressly includes changes made “as a result of model training or fine-tuning.” Pre-existing background IP, such as default configurations, is excluded.

  • Exclusive use. Custom Developments must be dedicated to the Government’s exclusive use and treated as Government confidential information. They cannot be reused for anyone else without written authorization from the contracting officer.
  • Deletion at the end. On completion, termination or expiration, the contractor must delete Government Data and Custom Developments, including fine-tuned model weights, embeddings, indexes and caches, and document the deletion in writing. A contracting officer can direct otherwise.

Improvements that touch government data are assigned

Paragraph (e)(1)(v) goes further than a licence. If the contractor gains IP rights in Government Data, or in improvements or derivative works that incorporate, reveal or disclose it, the contractor assigns those rights to the Government on creation.

The assignment has a stated limit. It “does not apply to general capability gains or generalized improvements that do not incorporate, derive from, reveal, or disclose Government Data.”

  • Prohibited uses. Paragraph (e)(3) bars training, fine-tuning or improving any LLM with Government Data. It also bars using the data for advertising or business decisions, retaining it beyond scope, passing it to unauthorized parties, and selling or licensing it.
  • Permitted uses. The contractor’s licence covers performing the work, de-identified security telemetry, support and other uses the contracting officer authorizes.

Portability turns ownership into something usable

Ownership matters little if data cannot be moved. Paragraph (h) requires export of Government Data in open or standard machine-readable formats, with the metadata and schema needed to migrate it.

The contractor also must not impose proprietary formats, technical restrictions, extra costs or licensing conditions that materially impair migration. It must provide tooling to migrate both Government Data and Custom Developments.

The FAR has no AI clause, and Part 27 is being rewritten

The FAR text in force on Acquisition.gov does not use the term “artificial intelligence” in Parts 12, 15, 27 or 52. The governing rule for commercial software remains FAR 12.212.

  • FAR 12.212(a). Commercial software is acquired under licences customarily provided to the public, to the extent they are consistent with federal law and meet the Government’s needs.
  • FAR 12.212(b). The Government has only the rights in the licence in the contract addendum.

That is why the GSA clause’s order-of-precedence paragraph matters. It places the clause in the “schedule of supplies/services” and says it takes precedence over conflicting commercial terms, including terms of service.

The proposed Part 27 would import the DFARS model

The FAR Council’s proposed rule on Parts 9, 27 and 47 (FAR Case 2026-011) was published September 18, 2026. It would delete FAR Subpart 27.4 and replace it with two DFARS-derived subparts, one for non-commercial and one for commercial items.

The preamble says the change would add “government purpose rights,” which the current FAR lacks. It also says the Government would get unlimited rights in eleven categories of data, compared with four under the current FAR.

  • Commercial software unchanged in principle. Proposed 27.502-1 keeps the customary-licence rule. Proposed 27.502-2 says no contract clause governs the Government’s rights in commercial computer software.
  • No AI-specific text. The proposed rule does not use the term “artificial intelligence.” Training data, model weights and outputs are not addressed by name.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Identify in your proposal what is Background Data and what will be Custom Development. The clause’s split depends on that line, and disputes start where it is vague.

  • Price the exclusivity. Fine-tuning for a GSA customer produces work you cannot reuse. Price it as dedicated work, not as a shared improvement.
  • Lead with portability. M-25-22 tells agencies to signal interest in proposals that reduce lock-in. Export formats and migration tooling can be evaluated strengths.

Compliance cost

The new costs come from engineering, not legal review. You need to prove where Government Data went and that it did not reach a training pipeline.

  • Data lineage. Track Government Data through retrieval indexes, caches and logs, because those must be deleted at the end.
  • Telemetry hygiene. Keep service metrics free of Government Data or Government Usage Context, so they stay outside the Data Outputs definition.

Execution

Commercial terms of service that grant a vendor broad rights to customer content conflict with the clause. The clause prevails on GSA orders where it applies.

  • Flow it down. The clause must flow to subcontractors that perform AI design, development, deployment or operation tasks and handle Government Data.
  • Watch other agencies. Outside GSA, terms depend on each agency’s M-25-22 process. Read each solicitation’s IP section; do not assume the GSA terms.

Watch next

  • Final Part 27 rule. Comments on FAR Case 2026-011 closed October 19, 2026. A final rule would change FAR data-rights clauses for civilian agencies.
  • Agency IP processes. M-25-22 required each agency to standardize its treatment of AI data ownership and IP. Watch for agency clauses that differ from GSA’s.
  • GSAR rulemaking. RGO-2026-01 states that formal GSAR rulemaking will follow the deviation.
