Buy American Content Rises to 75% in 2029, and the 55% Fallback Ends in 2030: The Schedule to Price In Now

Key takeaways

  • 65% now, 75% from 2029. Under FAR 25.101, a manufactured end product is domestic only if its domestic components cost more than 65% of all components for items delivered in 2024 through 2028. The figure rises to 75% for items delivered from calendar year 2029.
  • Delivery year controls. A contract spanning the increases must meet each higher threshold in the year of delivery. The exception is when the agency’s senior procurement executive approves locking in the award-date threshold, after consulting OMB’s Made in America Office.
  • The fallback ends in 2030. Until then, if no domestic offer is reasonable, a US-made product with more than 55% domestic content can be treated as domestic. FAR says that procedure “will no longer apply as of January 1, 2030.”
  • DoD counts allies. The DFARS uses the same 60/65/75% schedule but counts components from qualifying countries. It applies a 50% evaluation factor instead of the FAR’s 20% or 30%.
  • No critical items listed yet. FAR 25.105 provides for higher price preferences on critical items and components, but the list is still “[Reserved].”

Findings

The test has two parts, and the second one is rising

FAR 25.101(a) sets a two-part test for manufactured end products. The product must be made in the United States, and it must pass a domestic content test.

  • Iron and steel. For products wholly or mainly of iron or steel, foreign iron and steel must be less than 5% of component cost. Iron or steel components of unknown origin count as foreign.
  • COTS. The content test is waived for COTS items, except products wholly or mainly of iron or steel, other than COTS fasteners.
  • Unknown origin. The definition in FAR 52.225-1 says “Components of unknown origin are treated as foreign.”

Delivery date, not award date, sets the threshold

FAR 25.101(d)(1) says a contract that spans the increases must meet each higher threshold for items in their year of delivery. A five-year production contract awarded in 2027 will therefore face 75% on its 2029 deliveries.

The senior procurement executive may allow an alternate test, under which the award-date threshold applies for the whole contract. That authority cannot be delegated, and the executive must consult OMB’s Made in America Office first.

  • Clause signal. If the alternate test is allowed, the contract will use an Alternate of the Buy American clause, per FAR 25.101(d)(2). Check which version is in your contract.

The 55% fallback ends January 1, 2030

FAR 25.106(b)(2) provides a fallback for non-COTS end products that are not mainly iron or steel. It applies when the domestic offer is unreasonable in cost or there is none, and the low offer is foreign with 55% or less domestic content.

In that case, the contracting officer treats the lowest US-made foreign offer above 55% domestic content as domestic, and evaluates it against the low offer. Paragraph (b)(2)(iii) then ends it.

  • End date. “The procedures in this paragraph (b)(2) will no longer apply as of January 1, 2030.”
  • Construction too. FAR 25.204 has a parallel 55% rule for construction material that also no longer applies as of January 1, 2030.
  • DoD mirror. DFARS 225.202 says no nonavailability determination is needed before January 1, 2030, if there is an offer of foreign construction material above 55% domestic content.

Price preferences: 20% or 30%, and 50% at DoD

When a domestic offer is not the lowest, FAR 25.106(b)(1) adds a factor to the low foreign offer. It adds 20% if the lowest domestic offer is from a large business and 30% if from a small business.

DFARS 225.106 replaces those with 50% for DoD. FAR 25.106(a)(2) says the factors are not applied to eligible products where a trade agreement covers the acquisition.

  • Critical items. FAR 25.106(c) allows an extra preference for critical items and components listed at FAR 25.105. That list is currently “(1)-(2) [Reserved],” so no extra factor applies today.
  • List updates. FAR 25.105(b) says the list will be published for public comment at least every four years.

DoD uses the same schedule but counts qualifying countries

DFARS 225.101(a) applies the same 60/65/75% schedule. For DoD, the test counts “U.S. and qualifying country components,” and it is applied to end products, not to individual components.

DFARS 225.101(d)(1) lets the senior procurement executive approve the alternate award-date test after consulting the Made in America Office. The DoD rule implementing the schedule took effect February 15, 2024.

The FAR overhaul has not touched Part 25 yet

The FAR Council has published Revolutionary FAR Overhaul proposed rules for several parts. None of those found in the Federal Register through October 11, 2026 covers Part 25.

  • What that means. The statutory schedule above is the operative text. The FAR Council has said its twelve proposed rules would together streamline the FAR in its entirety, so a Part 25 rule may still come.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Price multiyear production against the delivery-year threshold, not the award-year one. Unless the contract uses the alternate test, deliveries from January 1, 2029, need 75% domestic content.

  • Ask early. In questions on a solicitation, ask whether the agency will request the alternate domestic content test for a long contract.
  • Plan beyond 2029. Offers that currently rely on the 55% fallback should assume it is gone for awards evaluated from January 1, 2030.

Compliance cost

The cost is supplier data. Components of unknown origin count as foreign, so gaps in supplier origin records reduce your domestic percentage.

  • Bill of materials. Keep component cost and origin at each tier that feeds the end product.
  • Iron and steel. Track foreign iron and steel content separately against the 5% limit.

Execution

Recalculate domestic content for each delivery year in long contracts. A product that passes at 65% in 2028 can fail at 75% in 2029 with no design change.

  • Sourcing lead time. Qualifying new domestic suppliers can take longer than the time left before 2029. Start now for products near the threshold.

Watch next

  • Jan 1, 2029. The 75% threshold applies to items delivered from this date.
  • Jan 1, 2030. The 55% fallback for end products and construction material ends.
  • Critical items list. Any FAR 25.105 list published for comment would add higher price preferences for named items.
  • Part 25 overhaul. Watch for the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul proposed rule covering Part 25.
