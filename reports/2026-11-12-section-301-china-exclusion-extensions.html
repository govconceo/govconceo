USTR Extends All 178 Current Section 301 China Product Exclusions

Key takeaways

  • All 178 current exclusions are extended. In a notice published November 10, 2026, USTR announced the Trade Representative’s determination to further extend all 178 current Section 301 exclusions on certain products of China.
  • The annex entry window. Annexes A and B make the extension effective for goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on November 10, 2026, and before 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027. The headings named are 9903.88.69 and 9903.88.70.
  • The notice also uses January 10. The determination paragraph says the Trade Representative determined it is appropriate to further extend all 178 current exclusions until January 10, 2027. The DATES line says the annexes extend the exclusions through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027. This report quotes both formulations and does not choose between them.
  • The prior expiration was November 9, 2026. The notice recites that on November 26, 2025, USTR announced an extension of all 178 current exclusions through November 9, 2026. The annexes delete “November 9, 2026,” from the article descriptions and insert “January 9, 2027.”
  • Further extensions remain possible. “The Trade Representative may continue to consider further extensions or additional modifications as appropriate.” U.S. Customs and Border Protection will issue instructions on entry guidance and implementation.

Findings

USTR extended all 178 current exclusions

The Office of the United States Trade Representative published the notice on November 10, 2026, at 91 FR 72610. It concerns the Section 301 investigation of China’s acts, policies, and practices related to technology transfer, intellectual property, and innovation.

The summary states the action directly:

The notice gives the reason as “ongoing discussions with China.” It says the decision also takes into account public comments on the September 16, 2025 invitation, earlier comments, advisory-committee advice, and the advice of the interagency Section 301 Committee.

The notice cites Section 307(a)(1) of the Trade Act of 1974, as amended (19 U.S.C. 2411–2418), as the authority to modify an action taken under Section 301.

The annexes set an entry window — and the notice also uses January 10

Annex A extends all exclusions under heading 9903.88.69 and U.S. notes 20(vvv)(i) through 20(vvv)(iv) to subchapter III of chapter 99 of the HTSUS. Annex B extends all exclusions under heading 9903.88.70 and U.S. note 20(www) to the same subchapter.

Effective November 10, 2026, both annexes modify the article description of the relevant heading by deleting “November 9, 2026,” and inserting “January 9, 2027.” The DATES line says the annexes “further extend the exclusions through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027.”

Separately, section B says the Trade Representative “has determined that it is appropriate to further extend all 178 current exclusions until January 10, 2027.” This report records that wording. It does not treat January 9 and January 10 as the same date, and it does not resolve the difference beyond quoting the notice.

Scope is the product description — this notice does not list the 178 items

The notice says the exclusion extensions in the annexes “are available for any product that meets the description in the product exclusion.” It also says the scope of each exclusion is governed by the ten-digit HTSUS statistical reporting numbers and product descriptions in the U.S. notes named above.

The November 10 notice does not reprint those product descriptions or a table of the 178 exclusions. Contractors who need to know whether a specific article qualifies have to use those HTSUS notes and descriptions, not this summary.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection “will issue instructions on entry guidance and implementation.” This report has not opened later CBP instructions. The Trade Representative “may continue to consider further extensions or additional modifications as appropriate.”

The notice’s account of how the 178 exclusions were last extended

The notice recites a longer exclusion history, including earlier 2018–2020 duty tranches and comment processes. For the current 178, it says USTR on May 30, 2024, extended 164 exclusions through May 31, 2025, and on September 18, 2024, announced fourteen exclusions covering certain solar manufacturing equipment.

It says those 164 plus fourteen were later extended through August 31, 2025, and then for 90 days through November 29, 2025. After a September 16, 2025 comment process, the notice recites a November 1, 2025 White House announcement of a trade and economic deal.

The notice says USTR on November 26, 2025, extended all 178 current exclusions through November 9, 2026.

Those earlier Federal Register pages are cited in the November 10 notice. This report uses that recital. It does not treat the older notices as independently re-opened for this draft.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

For proposals that assume Chinese-origin inputs covered by these exclusions, the landed-cost picture has changed from a November 9, 2026 expiration to the window stated in the annexes.

  • Price the window, not an open-ended exemption. The annexes describe a dated entry window, not a permanent removal of the additional duties.
  • Confirm the article matches the exclusion. Availability depends on the product description and the ten-digit HTSUS numbers in the notes the notice cites. This notice does not list those items.
  • Watch January. Bids with deliveries or entries near the January 9, 2027 time stated in the annexes should not assume a further extension until one is published.

Compliance cost

The notice does not state a dollar cost or a duty rate. The practical cost question is whether a given article stays inside an exclusion through the entry date, or falls back to the additional Section 301 duties.

  • Classification work remains. Matching an article to the HTSUS notes and product descriptions is still required.
  • CBP instructions are still to come. The notice says CBP will issue entry guidance and implementation instructions. Those instructions were not attached to this notice.

Execution

Import teams and proposal desks should align entry dates with the annex window and should track any later USTR modification.

  • Entry timing. The annex test is entry for consumption, or withdrawal from warehouse for consumption, inside the stated window.
  • Further action is possible. The Trade Representative may consider further extensions or additional modifications.
  • Do not collapse the two end-date formulations. Record both the annex/DATES language (January 9, 2027, 11:59 p.m. eastern time) and the determination’s “until January 10, 2027” wording until official guidance addresses the difference, if it does.

Watch next

  • CBP instructions. The notice says U.S. Customs and Border Protection will issue instructions on entry guidance and implementation.
  • A further extension or modification. The Trade Representative may continue to consider further extensions or additional modifications as appropriate. No later notice of that kind was opened for this report.
  • The January 9 / January 10 wording. Watch whether a later official instruction or notice addresses the two end-date formulations in 91 FR 72610.
