Year-End Spending: September Alone Was 19.5% of FY2025 Contract Obligations

Key takeaways

  • The fourth quarter keeps getting bigger. July–September made up 32.7% of governmentwide contract obligations in FY2023, 35.7% in FY2024 and 37.9% in FY2025.
  • September is the peak. It accounted for $161.9 billion (19.5%) of FY2025's $828.6 billion. That is more than double July ($75.2 billion) or August ($77.1 billion).
  • DoD follows the same pattern. Its Q4 share rose from 35.5% (FY2024) to 37.3% (FY2025). September FY2025 was $105.6 billion, or 19.5% of DoD's year.
  • The law permits a surge but sets limits. Annual funds must meet a need of that year (31 U.S.C. 1502). Severable services may run up to 12 months across years (41 U.S.C. 3902; 10 U.S.C. 3133).
  • DoD has a 20% rule for the last two months. It applies to its one-year funds (P.L. 119-75, Div. A, Sec. 8004). Aggregate USAspending data cannot test compliance with it.

Scope note: This report is narrowed to FY2023–FY2025. FY2026 data was incomplete in USAspending when retrieved, so it is excluded.

In FY2025, DoD's August and September together were 28.7% of its contract obligations. That figure mixes one-year and multiyear money, so it says nothing about compliance with the Sec. 8004 limit described below.

Rule 1: bona fide need

31 U.S.C. 1502(a) makes a time-limited appropriation available "only for payment of expenses properly incurred during the period of availability or to complete contracts properly made within that period of availability."

In practice, September obligations of one-year funds must meet a need of that fiscal year. A need of the next year does not qualify.

Rule 2: severable services can cross the year

Civilian agencies (41 U.S.C. 3902) and DoD and the Coast Guard (10 U.S.C. 3133) can contract for severable services "for a period that begins in one fiscal year and ends in the next fiscal year." The contract period cannot exceed one year, not counting options.

Funds of the first year "may be obligated for the total amount" of the contract. That is why a 12-month service contract awarded in September can be fully funded with expiring money.

Rule 3: DoD's last-two-months cap

The limit covers only appropriations in that Act "limited for obligation during the current fiscal year." Sec. 8003 says no appropriation in the Act remains available beyond the current year "unless expressly so provided." Multiyear procurement and RDT&E accounts fall outside the 20% test.

Rule 4: the Antideficiency Act

31 U.S.C. 1341(a)(1) bars an officer from obligating "exceeding an amount available in an appropriation." It also bars involving the government in a contract "before an appropriation is made unless authorized by law." A September surge is limited by available balances, not by demand.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Have September-ready awards. Keep proposals, pricing and SAM registration current by July. Most of Q4's dollars land in September.
  • Pitch 12-month severable service periods. These can be fully funded with expiring money under 41 U.S.C. 3902 or 10 U.S.C. 3133.
  • Offer clean modifications. Option exercises and in-scope additions are faster to obligate than new awards.

Compliance cost

  • Watch bona fide need. A year-end order for goods or services that actually belong to next year can be challenged. Document the current-year need in your quote.
  • Plan for audit. Fast, high-volume awards produce errors. Check clauses and funding lines on every September modification.

Execution

  • Staff for the surge. Have contracts and pricing staff available for late September and the first days of October.
  • Expect a slow Q1. In every year shown, October–December obligations were about half of Q4. FY2027 starts under a CR.

Watch next

  • FY2026 data maturity. Updated USAspending figures would show whether the FY2025 pattern held.
  • FY2027 full-year bills. The FY2027 DoD act would carry its own version of Sec. 8004, if included.
  • Fixed-price policy. EO 14402's justification requirement adds approval steps for non-fixed-price awards above its thresholds, which could affect year-end timing.
