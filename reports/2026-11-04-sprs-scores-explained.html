SPRS Scores Explained: What the 110-Point Number Means at Award, and Who Checks It

Key takeaways

  • No current score, no award. DFARS 252.204-7019 says an offeror that must implement NIST SP 800-171 must have a current assessment, not more than 3 years old, to be considered for award.
  • The contracting officer checks. DFARS 204.7303(b) requires the contracting officer to verify the SPRS summary score before award, before exercising an option, and before extending the period of performance.
  • Three assessment levels. A Basic Assessment is a self-assessment with "Low" confidence. Medium and High Assessments are conducted by the Government and carry "Medium" and "High" confidence.
  • The score is a summary. The clauses ask for a summary level score such as "95 out of 110", plus the date a score of 110 is expected. Individual requirement values are not reported.
  • Scores can be negative. The CMMC scoring rule at 32 CFR 170.24 subtracts 5, 3 or 1 points per unmet requirement, "which may result in a negative score." A DOJ settlement describes the range as -203 to 110.
  • A false score is FCA exposure. In March 2025 MORSECORP admitted it posted 104 in January 2021 when a consultant later put its score at -142. It paid $4.6 million.

Findings

A current SPRS score is a condition of award, not a nice-to-have

DFARS 252.204-7019 is a solicitation provision. Its paragraph (b) makes a current assessment a condition of being "considered for award" when the offeror must implement NIST SP 800-171.

"Current" means not more than 3 years old unless the solicitation specifies a shorter time. The requirement applies to each covered contractor information system relevant to the offer, contract, task order or delivery order.

If no current score is posted, paragraph (c)(2) lets the offeror conduct and submit a Basic Assessment for posting. The clause at 252.204-7020 gives the submission route as encrypted email to webptsmh@navy.mil.

The contracting officer must look before award, options and extensions

DFARS 204.7303(b) puts the check on the Government side. The contracting officer "shall verify" that a current summary score is posted in SPRS before three events:

  • Award. Awarding a contract, task order or delivery order to an offeror required to implement 800-171 under 252.204-7012.
  • Options. Exercising an option period.
  • Extensions. Extending the period of performance.

That makes an expired score a risk mid-contract, not only at proposal time. A score posted three years before an option date can lapse before the option is exercised.

Basic, Medium and High: who scores, and how much the Government trusts it

DFARS 252.204-7020(a) defines three levels of DoD assessment:

  • Basic. The contractor's self-assessment, based on its review of its system security plan(s) and conducted under the NIST SP 800-171 DoD Assessment Methodology. It results in a confidence level of "Low" because it is self-generated.
  • Medium. Conducted by the Government. It reviews the Basic Assessment, includes a thorough document review and discussions with the contractor. Confidence is "Medium".
  • High. Conducted by Government personnel using NIST SP 800-171A. It adds verification, examination and demonstration of the system security plan. Confidence is "High".

Paragraph (c) requires the contractor to give the Government access to facilities, systems and personnel for a Medium or High Assessment, if necessary.

What goes into SPRS is a summary, tied to a plan date

For a Basic Assessment, 252.204-7020(d)(1) lists what the email must contain:

  • Standard. The version of NIST SP 800-171 assessed.
  • Assessor. The organization conducting the assessment, for example a contractor self-assessment.
  • Scope. For each system security plan, all associated CAGE codes and, if there is more than one plan, a brief description of the plan architecture.
  • Date. The date the assessment was completed.
  • Score. The summary level score, for example "95 out of 110", "NOT the individual value for each requirement".
  • Completion date. The date all requirements are expected to be implemented, meaning a score of 110, based on the plans of action.

DoD posts Medium and High results itself, with the assessing organization, the level and the date. Scores are posted within 30 days of the assessment. Under 7019(d)(3), they are available to DoD personnel and protected under DoD Instruction 5000.79, and a contractor's representatives can see only their own scores.

How the 110-point scale works

The DoD Assessment Methodology that 7019 and 7020 cite could not be opened for this report. The published CMMC scoring rule at 32 CFR 170.24 sets out a scale built on the same 110 NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 requirements:

  • Start at the maximum. Each requirement NOT MET subtracts its value from the maximum score.
  • 5 points. Requirements that, if not implemented, "could lead to significant exploitation of the network, or exfiltration of CUI." The rule lists them, including 3.1.1, 3.5.1, 3.6.1 and 3.13.1.
  • 3 points. Requirements with a "specific and confined effect", such as 3.3.2 and 3.12.2.
  • 1 point. All remaining derived requirements.
  • Partial credit. Multi-factor authentication (3.5.3) costs 3 points if used only for remote and privileged users and 5 if not used at all. Encryption (3.13.11) costs 3 points if not FIPS-validated and 5 if absent.

The rule says this subtraction "may result in a negative score." In its March 2025 MORSECORP release, DOJ described the possible range as "-203 to 110."

SPRS also feeds evaluation risk ratings

DFARS 252.204-7024 (MAR 2023) is a separate provision. It tells offerors that SPRS risk assessments will be used in evaluation.

The contracting officer considers item risk, price risk and supplier risk, including quality and delivery. The provision says SPRS risk assessments are generated daily and that the user's guide explains how to challenge a rating.

A score is a representation, and DOJ treats it as one

MORSECORP agreed to pay $4.6 million in a settlement DOJ announced on March 26, 2025. As part of it, the company admitted that it submitted a score of 104 in January 2021, "near the top of the possible score range from -203 to 110."

In July 2022, a third-party consultant told the company its score was actually -142. MORSECORP did not update SPRS until June 2023, three months after the Government served it with a subpoena.

In October 2024, Penn State paid $1.25 million. DOJ alleged that it posted scores showing unimplemented controls but "misrepresented the dates by which it would implement them."

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Check SPRS before the proposal, not after. A missing or expired score can make an offer ineligible for award under 7019(b).
  • Read the solicitation for a shorter window. The 3-year limit applies "unless a lesser time is specified in the solicitation."
  • Map CAGE codes to plans. Every CAGE code supporting the offer needs to be covered by a scored system security plan.

Compliance cost

  • The completion date matters. The plan date in SPRS is part of what you represent. Penn State's settlement turned in part on those dates.
  • Fix the score when it changes. MORSECORP's admitted facts include a correction made 11 months after it learned the real score.
  • Budget for Government access. A Medium or High Assessment requires access to facilities, systems and personnel.

Execution

  • Track expiry dates against option dates. 204.7303(b) applies before options and extensions.
  • Keep score, SSP and plans of action aligned. The Basic Assessment is defined as based on the system security plan.
  • Push the requirement down. Under 252.204-7020(g), primes may not award a subcontract subject to 7012 unless the subcontractor has a current Basic Assessment. See the companion flow-down report.

Watch next

  • CMMC Phase 2 status. Phase 2 is suspended under the July 13, 2026 memo. Only Level 1 (Self) and Level 2 (Self) may be required. See the GovConCEO report on the suspension.
  • Revision 3. NIST lists SP 800-171 Rev 2 as withdrawn and superseded by Rev 3 (May 14, 2024). The DFARS and 32 CFR 170 texts opened for this report still reference Rev 2.
  • New settlements. DOJ's most recent cyber-fraud resolution opened for this report is ILC Dover (Nov 3, 2026), which cited the lack of a current assessment.
