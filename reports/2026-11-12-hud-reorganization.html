HUD Will Reorganize Its Operating Model, Including a Centralized Early-Acquisition Hub at OCPO

Key takeaways

  • HUD has published a reorganization plan. A November 10, 2026 notice advises that HUD intends to change its Headquarters and Field operational structure. The notice is a statutory cost-benefit publication under section 7(p) of the Department of Housing and Urban Development Act (42 U.S.C. 3535(p)).
  • No office closures or staff relocation are proposed. The summary states: “It does not propose any office closures or relocation of staff.” The cost-savings section says the plans include no steps “to relocate or reduce any staff.”
  • OCPO will be re-centered on early acquisition. The Office of the Chief Procurement Officer “will restructure its operations to establish a centralized hub for early acquisition life-cycle activities, consolidating the presently dispersed functions.”
  • Timing is “approximately 90 days” after publication, not a calendar date named in the notice. “Approximately 90 days following the date of publication of this notice, HUD will begin the reorganization of the Department as identified above.” The notice does not name a February date.
  • Not every change is tied to the 90-day clock. The notice says not all described changes “rise to the level legally requiring a 90-day notice period and may be implemented by the Department at its convenience.” Full implementation is “expected to occur within Fiscal Year 2026 for some offices while others will continue working towards full implementation in Fiscal Year 2027.”

Findings

HUD published a plan, not a closure list

The Department of Housing and Urban Development, Office of the Secretary, published “Notice of Intent To Reorganize HUD and Revise Headquarters and Field Operating Model” on November 10, 2026, at 91 FR 72551 (Docket No. FR-6623-N-01). It was signed by Deputy Secretary Andrew Hughes.

The summary says the changes are designed to eliminate redundancies, streamline work processes, and consolidate administrative functions, “thus allowing the Department to more efficiently deliver on its mission utilizing current resources.”

The notice says these efforts are about streamlining and consolidating organizational structures and reducing redundancies in terms of functions performed “rather than any change in terms of footprint or staffing levels,” and that “nothing in these plans includes steps to relocate or reduce any staff.”

It also says immediate, quantifiable cost savings “are not expected to be realized” from the plan, and that HUD anticipates “greater efficiencies in qualitative operations.”

A footnote to that staff sentence states that the Department “separately published a notice of intent to relocate the staff from Kansas City, Missouri field office to the Kansas City, Kansas Regional Office.” It says that move “is separate and distinct from the reorganization plans discussed in this notice.” This report has not opened that separate notice.

OCPO will become a centralized early-acquisition hub

For contractors, the procurement-office passage is the operational point. It appears under “Office of Administration,” item d:

The same paragraph says additional modernization “will require establishment of various branches across OCPO designed to further the efficient management of procurement activities and establish greater communication and ties with the program offices across HUD.”

The notice does not name those branches, does not identify a new contracting activity, and does not say that existing HUD contracts will be transferred or terminated. This report is relevant mainly to firms that sell to HUD, and it does not treat the notice as a governmentwide buying-office change.

The notice also names other HUD offices, and two contractor-adjacent details

The contractor-facing change is the OCPO hub. The notice’s “Description of Proposed Changes” also covers other components. This report lists those headings as the notice names them and does not inventory each internal realignment.

  • Departmental Management: Executive Secretariat and the Office of Public Affairs.
  • Program and support offices: Public and Indian Housing; Lead Hazard Control and Healthy Homes; Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity; the Office of the Chief Financial Officer; the Office of the Chief Information Officer; Community Planning and Development; the Government National Mortgage Association (Ginnie Mae); Policy Development and Research; the Office of General Counsel; and Field Policy and Management.
  • Office of Housing—Federal Housing Administration: Office of Single Family Housing and Office of Operations.
  • Office of Administration, besides OCPO: the Executive Office, the Office of the Chief Human Capital Officer, and the Office of the Chief Administrative Officer.

Two passages are more likely to matter to firms that already work with HUD programs:

  • Build America, Buy America. The Office of Field Policy and Management “will realign the additional cross-cutting program implementation functions associated with the Build America, Buy America Act, into the office that currently oversees various cross-programmatic compliance initiatives for the Department.”
  • Single Family Homeownership Centers. The Office of Single Family Housing “is transitioning the work of its Homeownership Centers (HOCs) from a geographic-based business model to a function-based business model.” The notice says Single Family “will maintain three official HOC-designated offices, each with its own specialization: Philadelphia (Origination), Denver (Servicing), and Atlanta (Quality Assurance).”
  • Santa Ana. “Santa Ana will no longer be identified as a HOC, however all Single Family office locations, including Santa Ana, will continue to remain in operation, with no geographic reassignments, and minimal changes to employee position descriptions.”

When the reorganization begins, as the notice states it

Section 7(p) of the Department of Housing and Urban Development Act, 42 U.S.C. 3535(p), as quoted in the notice, provides that a plan to reorganize any HUD regional, area, insuring, or other field office “may take effect only upon the expiration of 90 days after publication in the Federal Register of a cost-benefit analysis.”

The notice says it is “to satisfy the requirement of section 7(p).”

The notice then qualifies that clock: “Not all changes described in this notice rise to the level legally requiring a 90-day notice period and may be implemented by the Department at its convenience.” Those changes “are being described here to provide a full picture of the future organizational structure of the Department.”

On start of the overall plan, the notice says: “Approximately 90 days following the date of publication of this notice, HUD will begin the reorganization of the Department as identified above.” Publication was November 10, 2026. The notice does not convert that “approximately 90 days” into a calendar date, and this report does not add one.

Each program office “will separately undertake the necessary reorganization, consolidation, and realignment of functions and staff.”

Full implementation “is expected to occur within Fiscal Year 2026 for some offices while others will continue working towards full implementation in Fiscal Year 2027.” Earlier in the same notice, HUD also writes: “The goal is to begin implementing these changes by the end of fiscal year (FY) 2026, with full implementation completed during FY 2027.”

The notice does not further define those fiscal-year references. Implementation “will be phased to ensure continuity of operation.”

How this sits with the October 13 reorganizations report

An earlier GovConCEO report, Agency Reorganizations and Your Contracts: Five Executive Orders in Force, a Budget Full of Proposals, reviewed Executive Orders and Budget proposals then in the official texts opened for that report. This November 10 HUD notice is a later, department-specific plan. It should be read as HUD’s published notice, not as a restatement of those earlier sources.

This report does not repeat claims from that earlier piece that were not verified there, and it does not treat the HUD notice as confirming governmentwide reorganization results beyond what 91 FR 72551 itself states.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

The practical audience is firms that market to HUD. The notice points to a centralized OCPO hub for early acquisition life-cycle work and closer ties to program offices. It does not announce a new vehicle, a paused solicitation calendar, or a change in how existing awards will be administered.

  • Expect a different HUD entry point, not a new governmentwide rule. The stated change is internal structure, including OCPO.
  • Do not price a closure or relocation shock. The notice proposes neither office closures nor relocation of staff.

Compliance cost

The notice does not impose a new FAR or HUDAR clause, and it does not quantify contractor cost. It also says HUD does not expect immediate, quantifiable cost savings of its own from the plan.

  • No new representation is stated here. Nothing in the opened notice adds a certification or reporting duty on contractors.
  • Continuity is the official theme. Implementation is to be phased “to ensure continuity of operation.”

Execution

HUD contractors should watch for later official indications of who handles pre-award work once OCPO’s “centralized hub” and new branches are stood up. The start of the overall reorganization is described as approximately 90 days after November 10, 2026, with the added caveat that some described changes may be implemented sooner at HUD’s convenience.

  • Do not invent a start date. Use the notice’s “approximately 90 days following the date of publication” language unless a later official document names a date.
  • Track HUD-specific communications. The notice does not itself amend existing contracts.

Watch next

  • The approximately-90-day mark after November 10, 2026. That is when the notice says HUD will begin the reorganization described there, subject to the “at its convenience” qualification for changes that do not require the statutory 90-day period.
  • Any later HUD acquisition notice or industry communication that names the OCPO branches or changes the contracting point of contact. No such follow-on notice was opened for this report.
  • The Fiscal Year 2026 / 2027 implementation statement. The notice uses both fiscal-year references and does not further define them.
