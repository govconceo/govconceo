How Continuing Resolutions Work: New Starts, Funding Rates and What They Do to Awards

Key takeaways

  • A CR funds last year's activities at last year's rate. P.L. 119-103 Sec. 101 continues FY2026 projects and activities "at a rate for operations" set by the FY2026 appropriations acts, under the same conditions.
  • No new starts. Sec. 104 bars using CR funds to "initiate or resume" any project or activity that had no FY2026 funding.
  • DoD has tighter limits. Sec. 102 bars new production, production-rate increases and new multiyear procurements using economic order quantity advance funding.
  • Agencies must spend cautiously. Sec. 110 requires "only the most limited funding action" needed to continue projects and activities.
  • Anomalies are the exceptions. Specific sections override the new-start rules, such as $2.853 billion for DoD National Security Systems (Sec. 127).
  • It expires Dec 11, 2026 unless a full-year bill or another CR is enacted first (Sec. 106).

For the current status of the CR, see GovConCEO's report FY2027 Is Running on a Continuing Resolution Through December 11.

Findings

The funding rate: last year's level, last year's rules

Sec. 101 provides "such amounts as may be necessary, at a rate for operations as provided in the applicable appropriations Acts for fiscal year 2026." It covers activities "that were conducted in fiscal year 2026."

The FY2026 acts it lists include division B of P.L. 119-37 (Agriculture), division A of P.L. 119-74 (Commerce, Justice, Science) and the FY2026 Department of Defense Appropriations Act. Sec. 103 makes funds available "to the extent and in the manner" the FY2026 act would have.

The CR does not create new funding levels. It continues FY2026 funding for a limited period.

The new-start ban

For contractors, this means a program that is new in the FY2027 request cannot start under the CR unless an anomaly allows it.

DoD's three extra limits

Sec. 102(a) bars DoD from using CR funds for:

  • New production. "The new production of items not funded for production in fiscal year 2026 or prior years."
  • Rate increases. "The increase in production rates above those sustained with fiscal year 2026 funds."
  • New starts, defined narrowly. Starting or resuming any project or activity without FY2026 funding. "Project or activity" is defined down to program element, subprogram, P-1 line item and R-1 line item.

Sec. 102(b) adds that DoD may not "initiate multi-year procurements utilizing advance procurement funding for economic order quantity procurement unless specifically appropriated later."

Spend cautiously, and no front-loading

  • Most limited action. Sec. 110: the Act "shall be implemented so that only the most limited funding action of that permitted in the Act shall be taken in order to provide for continuation of projects and activities."
  • No high initial rates. Sec. 109 bars programs with high early-year rates of operation from making those distributions. It also bars grants "that would impinge on final funding prerogatives."
  • Furlough avoidance comes after cuts elsewhere. Sec. 112 lets agencies apportion pay and benefits up to the rate needed to avoid furloughs. They can do so only after reducing or deferring "non-personnel-related administrative expenses."

Duration and what happens next

Sec. 106 makes CR funds available until the first of three events:

  • Enactment of an appropriation for the project or activity.
  • Enactment of the full-year act without it. The FY2027 act is enacted with no provision for the project or activity.
  • Expiration. December 11, 2026.

Sec. 107 charges spending under the CR to the final appropriation once that bill is enacted. Sec. 105 says CR funds cover obligations incurred while the CR is in force.

Anomalies: the exceptions that matter

Several sections begin "Notwithstanding sections 102 and 104." Examples from the act:

  • Shipbuilding (Sec. 126). Navy "Shipbuilding and Conversion" funds may be apportioned to cover prior-year cost increases. These include up to $324,000,000 for the 2013/2027 Carrier Replacement Program and up to $164,300,000 for the 2017/2027 LHA Replacement Program.
  • National Security Systems (Sec. 127). "Procurement, Defense-Wide" may be apportioned up to the rate needed for National Security Systems, not to exceed $2,853,000,000.
  • Military construction (Sec. 146). Army and Navy military construction funds may be used for unaccompanied housing at the Medical Education Training Complex, Joint Base San Antonio.

If your program is not named in an anomaly, assume the general rules apply.

The FAR tools for starting work before full-year funds

Codified FAR 32.703-2(a) lets a contracting officer "initiate a contract action properly chargeable to funds of the new fiscal year before these funds are available." The contract must include the clause at 52.232-18, Availability of Funds.

That authority covers only operation and maintenance and continuing services that are "necessary for normal operations" and that Congress "had consistently appropriated funds" for. Clause 52.232-18 states:

  • No liability yet. "No legal liability on the part of the Government for any payment may arise until funds are made available to the Contracting Officer for this contract."
  • Written notice. Funds are not available until "the Contractor receives notice of such availability, to be confirmed in writing."

Agencies working under RFO deviations may have revised Part 32 text. Check the version in your solicitation.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Separate new starts from continuing work. Opportunities tied to programs that are new in FY2027 are likely to slip until a full-year act passes or an anomaly covers them.
  • Read the anomaly list. Search the CR for your program, line item or account before assuming a delay.
  • Expect incremental awards. Under Sec. 110, contracting officers may fund options or modifications in smaller increments.

Compliance cost

  • Do not perform on unfunded work. Under 52.232-18, the government has no payment liability until you receive written notice that funds are available.
  • Track funding by modification. Each incremental funding action sets how far you can perform without risk.

Execution

  • Plan for Dec 11. If another CR follows, the same rules continue. A lapse would end the funding.
  • Keep DoD production at current rates. Sec. 102 bars rate increases above FY2026 levels.

Watch next

  • Dec 11, 2026 expiration. A full-year bill, another CR or a lapse.
  • OMB rescissions list. Due to the Appropriations Committees by Nov 20, 2026 (Sec. 115(c)).
  • Anomalies in any follow-on CR. New exceptions would appear as "notwithstanding sections 102 and 104" provisions.
