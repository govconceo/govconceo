USTR Extends the Vietnam Section 301 Investigation; Determinations Due by February 28, 2027

Key takeaways

  • The Vietnam Section 301 investigation is extended three months. A USTR notice published November 10, 2026, and effective November 3, 2026, at 12:01 a.m. EDT, extends the ongoing investigation of certain acts, policies, and practices of the Government of Vietnam related to intellectual property protection and enforcement.
  • Determinations are due by February 28, 2027. “The determinations in the investigation will be made by no later than February 28, 2027.”
  • The ground cited is complexity, not a finding on Vietnam’s legislation or enforcement. The Trade Representative determined that “the investigation involves complex or complicated issues that require additional time.” The notice does not, in the text opened, state that Vietnam is making substantial progress on legislation or is undertaking enforcement measures — the other statutory grounds it also recites.
  • This notice does not impose duties or publish an exclusion list. It extends the time for findings on actionability and what action, if any, to take. It is a watch item, not an immediate landed-cost change.

Findings

USTR added three months to the Vietnam IP investigation

The Office of the United States Trade Representative published “Notice of Determination To Extend Section 301 Investigation of Vietnam’s Acts, Policies, and Practices Related to Intellectual Property Protection and Enforcement” on November 10, 2026, at 91 FR 72609 (Docket No. USTR-2026-0364). It was signed by General Counsel Jennifer Thornton.

The DATES line says the notice “is effective as of Tuesday, November 3, 2026, at 12:01 a.m. EDT.” The notice does not, in the text opened, announce additional duties, a tariff list, an exclusion process, or a negotiated suspension of the investigation.

The statutory clock, as the notice recites it

The notice says the investigation was initiated on May 29, 2026, under Section 302(b)(2) of the Trade Act of 1974, as amended, and points readers to the initiation notice at 91 FR 33285 (June 3, 2026). This report uses that recital and does not treat the June 3 notice as a separately re-opened source for additional facts.

For investigations initiated under that section that do not involve a trade agreement, the notice recites that Section 304(a)(3)(A) directs the Trade Representative to make findings on actionability and what action, if any, to take within six months of the date of initiation.

It then recites Section 304(a)(3)(B). If the Trade Representative determines any of the following, the findings are to be made within nine months of the date of initiation:

  • Complexity. The investigation “involves complex or complicated issues that require additional time.”
  • Legislation. The foreign country “is making substantial progress in drafting or implementing legislative or administrative measures that will provide adequate and effective protection of IP rights.”
  • Enforcement. The foreign country “is undertaking enforcement measures to provide adequate and effective protection of IP rights.”

The ground used is complexity; the due date is February 28, 2027

The notice is explicit about which of those grounds was used. “Pursuant to this provision, the Trade Representative has determined that the investigation involves complex or complicated issues that require additional time.”

The notice does not, in the text opened, make a determination on the other two Section 304(a)(3)(B) grounds it recites. It also does not say what action, if any, will be taken in February. Those questions remain for the later determinations.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

This is a watch item for companies with Vietnam-sourced content, not a change in duty rates. The notice extends the time for USTR to make findings on actionability and what action, if any, to take. It does not itself change the landed cost of Vietnamese-origin goods.

  • Do not price a new 301 duty from this notice. No additional duty or product list is published here.
  • Do not treat February 28, 2027, as a duty date. It is the date by which determinations will be made, including the possibility of no action.

Compliance cost

The notice does not add a certification, reporting, or exclusion-request process. There is no immediate compliance expenditure stated in the official text.

  • Monitoring is the work. Keep the docket (USTR-2026-0364) and the February 28, 2027 determination date on the trade-risk calendar.

Execution

Supply-chain and proposal files should record that the investigation remains open, that the extension is effective November 3, 2026, and that later official action — if any — would appear in a subsequent determination, not in this extension notice.

  • Vietnam sourcing is not, by this notice, newly restricted. It also is not cleared in perpetuity. The determinations are still ahead.

Watch next

  • February 28, 2027. The date the notice sets for findings on actionability and what action, if any, to take.
  • Any earlier official determination or request for comment in docket USTR-2026-0364. None later than this November 10 notice was opened for this report.
