Small Business and FAR Part 19: Deviation in Force, No Proposed Rule Yet, and Three SBA Rules Moving

Key takeaways

  • No proposed rule for Part 19. Part 19 is not in any of the eight published Revolutionary FAR Overhaul proposed rules. The operative overhaul text is the model deviation, issued Sep 26, 2025 and updated Feb 20, 2026.
  • The rule of two is stated plainly. Model 19.104-1(a) says that above the micro-purchase threshold, the contracting officer "must set the contract aside" when two or more competitive, responsible small businesses are expected to offer.
  • Order set-asides are discretionary and not protestable on that basis. Model 19.111-2 lets contracting officers set aside orders under multiple-award contracts at their discretion. The decision "is ... not a basis for protest."
  • eSRS is gone. GSA's supplement says eSRS.gov was decommissioned Feb 20, 2026 and subcontract reporting moved to SAM.gov. The Individual Subcontract Report deadline went from 30 to 45 days.
  • SBA changed 8(a). A final rule effective Sep 10, 2026 removes the presumption of social disadvantage for individually owned firms. Entity-owned firms are unaffected.
  • Size standards are open for comment. SBA proposed new size standards for 338 industries. Comments close Nov 20, 2026.

Findings

Part 19 runs on deviations, not a proposed rule

The FAR Council says it is issuing twelve RFO proposed rules. The eight published so far cover 36 parts, and Part 19 is not one of them.

Acquisition.gov lists the Part 19 model deviation as issued Sep 26, 2025 and updated Feb 20, 2026. At GSA, the class deviation RFO-2025-19 took effect Nov 3, 2025. It directs the GSA workforce to follow the model text "instead of FAR part 19 as codified."

Each agency adopts the model text through its own deviation. Contractors should check which version a given solicitation uses.

The rule of two, in the model text

Model 19.104-1(a) sets the test for total small business set-asides above the micro-purchase threshold. The contracting officer "must set the contract aside for small business if there is a reasonable expectation of obtaining offers" that are:

  • From two or more. Two or more responsible small business concerns.
  • Competitive. Competitive "in terms of fair market prices, quality, and delivery."

Model 19.104-1(b) puts the determination with the contracting officer, who "must document the reason" when a contract is not set aside as required. If SBA recommends a set-aside and the contracting officer rejects it, 19.102(f) applies.

Orders under multiple-award contracts

Model 19.111-2(a)(1) cites section 1331 of the Small Business Jobs Act of 2010 (15 U.S.C. 644(r)(2)). Contracting officers "may, at their discretion, set aside orders placed under multiple-award contracts" for the small business types listed in 19.000(a)(3).

The same paragraph keeps protests that "would otherwise be authorized on a separate basis" under 16.505(a)(10)(i), the order-protest rules.

Model 19.111-1 keeps the size rule for orders. An order takes the NAICS code and size standard of the contract, or of the contract portion it is placed against.

Reserves and the tool list

Model 19.000 lists the tools in one place.

  • Reserves. Contracting officers may use reserves on multiple-award contracts for any of the small business types (19.112).
  • HUBZone preference. They "must use" the HUBZone price evaluation preference in full and open competitions (19.110).
  • Subcontracting plans. They "must require" a subcontracting plan in certain acquisitions (19.109 and 19.302-1).

The model text keeps two safeguards. Equal low bids between a small and an other-than-small business go to the small business (19.203). A nonresponsible apparent small business winner is referred to SBA for a certificate of competency (19.204).

Subcontract reporting moved to SAM.gov

GSA's RFO-2025-19 Supplement 1, signed by Senior Procurement Executive Jeffrey A. Koses on Feb 20, 2026, made these changes:

  • eSRS. GSA "will decommission" eSRS.gov on Feb 20, 2026. Subcontracting reporting "will transition to SAM.Gov."
  • Reviews. It removes prescriptive manual steps for reviewing, approving or rejecting subcontract reports. The new system applies business rules to prevent common errors.
  • ISR deadline. It extends Individual Subcontract Reports from 30 to 45 days after the reporting period, "to align the FAR to SBA’s current regulations."

SBA: 8(a) eligibility for individually owned firms

SBA's final rule of Aug 11, 2026 (91 FR 51568), effective Sep 10, 2026, removes from 13 CFR 124.103 "the rebuttable presumption that individuals belonging to certain designated groups are socially disadvantaged." It sets revised standards for showing social disadvantage.

The rule "applies only to the 8(a) BD eligibility of small businesses owned and controlled by individuals." It does not affect firms owned by tribes, Alaska Native Corporations, Native Hawaiian Organizations or Community Development Corporations. It applies to all pending applications of individually owned applicants as of Sep 10.

SBA: program-wide rule and size standards

Two further SBA actions affect small business eligibility:

  • "Removing Constitutional Concerns From SBA Programs." This final rule (91 FR 61295), effective Sep 29, 2026, removes "certain unnecessary and potentially unlawful regulatory provisions requiring affirmative action by SBA program applicants or recipients."
  • Size standards. SBA proposed new size standards for 338 industry groups and industries on Aug 20, 2026 (91 FR 53741), with a revised methodology (91 FR 54096). On Sep 24 it extended comments 60 days, to Nov 20, 2026 (91 FR 60524).

Changes to size standards would change which firms qualify as small for a given NAICS code. That affects set-aside eligibility directly.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Market research is the lever. The rule of two turns on a "reasonable expectation" of two competitive small offers. Respond to sources-sought notices.
  • Order set-asides are discretionary. On multiple-award vehicles, you cannot protest a decision not to set aside an order on that ground alone. Engage before the order is issued.
  • Check your NAICS under the proposal. Look up your codes in SBA's 338-industry proposal before Nov 20.

Compliance cost

  • Subcontract reporting. Large primes report in SAM.gov now, with 45 days for ISRs.
  • 8(a) applicants. Individually owned applicants must make an individual showing of social disadvantage under the revised standards.

Execution

  • Comment by Nov 20, 2026. This is the only open SBA comment period covered here.
  • Watch for the Part 19 proposed rule. It would be one of the four remaining RFO rules.
  • Read the deviation in your contract. Part 19 text differs between agencies that adopted the model deviation and those still on the codified FAR.

Watch next

  • Part 19 proposed rule. Not published as of Oct 15, 2026.
  • Final size standards. These would follow the Nov 20 comment close. No date has been announced.
  • FAR conforming changes. FAR text implementing the SBA 8(a) and program-wide rules was not found in the sources opened.
