Defense vs. Civilian in the FY2027 Request: Defense Up 42.1%, Non-Defense Down 10.0%

Key takeaways

  • Defense function 050 rises $445.4 billion (+42.1%). It goes from $1,058.2 billion enacted in FY2026 to $1,503.5 billion requested for FY2027 in base budget authority, including mandatory resources.
  • Non-defense falls $73.4 billion (−10.0%). It goes from $733.5 billion to $660.1 billion.
  • Part of the defense increase is mandatory, not appropriated. The War line "includes mandatory resources." OMB notes a proposed "additional $350 billion in mandatory funding for DOW."
  • Seven lines grow. War, Justice, NNSA, VA, Transportation, DOE overall and GSA.
  • The deepest percentage cuts are at SBA (−67.2%), NSF (−54.7%), EPA (−52.4%), State and International (−30.4%), the Corps of Engineers (−28.3%), Labor (−25.9%) and NASA (−23.0%).
  • This is a request. FY2027 is running on a CR at FY2026 rates through Dec 11, 2026 (P.L. 119-103).

The total grows less than the base because non-base funding falls from $98.8 billion to $15.3 billion. That category includes emergency requirements and Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act funding.

Mandatory money is inside the defense number

The same footnote says the Working Families Tax Cut Act (P.L. 119-21) provided $157 billion in defense function 050 funds in 2025. Table S-3 shows those funds as estimated obligations in 2025 and 2026, "as most of the funds are intended for use in 2026."

For contractors, part of the defense increase depends on mandatory legislation, not only on the annual defense appropriations bill.

Where defense dollars sit

  • Department of War, including mandatory resources. $1,009.1 billion → $1,450.0 billion (+$440.9 billion, +43.7%).
  • NNSA, including mandatory resources. $29.2 billion → $32.8 billion (+$3.6 billion, +12.4%).

Caveats OMB itself flags

  • DHS baseline. The FY2026 column for DHS reflects annualized continuing appropriations, because a final FY2026 Homeland Security bill had not been enacted when the budget was prepared.
  • Wildland fire. The FY2025 and FY2026 levels for Agriculture and Interior include "notional adjustments" for the proposed U.S. Wildland Fire Service within Interior.
  • Rounding. "Some totals may not add due to rounding."

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Defense pipeline. The request points to growth across DoD and NNSA. Part of it depends on the proposed $350 billion in mandatory funding.
  • Civilian pipeline. Expect pressure at State, NSF, EPA, NASA, Labor and SBA. Treat the request as a direction, not a final number.
  • Growth pockets. VA, Justice and Transportation are the civilian lines that rise.

Compliance cost

  • Recompete risk. Programs facing cuts are more likely to be descoped, delayed or not exercised at option time. Track option dates against the appropriations outcome.

Execution

  • Plan on CR rates first. Until full-year bills pass, agencies spend at FY2026 rates, with no new starts (P.L. 119-103 Sec. 104).
  • Watch the mandatory vehicle. For defense, also track any legislation providing the proposed mandatory funds.

Watch next

  • FY2027 appropriations bills before or after the Dec 11, 2026 CR expiration.
  • Mandatory defense funding. Any reconciliation or other legislation carrying the proposed $350 billion.
  • Agency budget justifications. These break the toplines into programs and accounts.
