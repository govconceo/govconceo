GSA Issues MAS Refresh 34, Removing the 20-Year Contract Limit and Adding an LLM Safeguarding Clause

Key takeaways

  • GSA has issued MAS Refresh 34. eOffer lists the consolidated Multiple Award Schedule solicitation 47QSMD20R0001 as “Refresh # 0034,” published November 12, 2026. The issued SF30 attachment is labeled Refresh 0034, with a schedule refresh-mod creation date of November 12, 2026, and contains a “Summary of Significant Changes.”
  • Existing contractors have a 90-day acceptance window. The SF30 says the mass modification applying Refresh 34 “is to be accepted by the subject contractor no later than 90 days after the modification has been issued.” The SF30 does not print a calendar deadline.
  • New orders, including BPA calls, take the new terms; earlier orders do not. Changes apply to all new task and delivery orders issued after the modification’s effective date, including orders issued under existing BPAs. Orders issued before that date remain governed by the terms in effect at award.
  • The 20-year limit on schedule duration is removed. Under GSAR Overhaul Wave 4, the SF30 lists “Removal of the 20 year limitation on contract duration (see 552.238-116).” It also records deletion of 41 GSAR provisions/clauses, removal of eight FAS FSS clauses, and addition of five new GSAR clauses (552.238-190 to -194).
  • Two contractor-facing additions sit in the same refresh: optional FSS domestic-end-product certificate 552.238-130, which GSA says it will not enforce until OMB clears the information collection; and LLM safeguarding clause 552.239-7001. The refresh also describes an intended, not completed, removal of certain nitrile-glove items, removal of the HVAC Maintenance subgroup, and a phase-out of the voluntary HACS Incident Response subgroup at the end of Fiscal Year 2027.

Findings

GSA issued Refresh 34 on November 12

GSA’s eOffer published-solicitations page lists the consolidated Multiple Award Schedule solicitation 47QSMD20R0001 as “Refresh # 0034,” published November 12, 2026. That listing was still the current published MAS solicitation when opened on November 16, 2026.

The issued package includes an “SF30 Attachment” (eOffer document 16). The file is named “SF30 Attachment for Refresh Mod 47QSMD20R0001.pdf.” Its first page identifies Solicitation Number 47QSMD20R0001, Refresh Number 0034, and “Schedule Refresh Mod Creation Date : 2026-11-12,” and it is marked “Created on November 12, 2026.” The same attachment contains the official “SUMMARY OF SIGNIFICANT CHANGES.”

This report is based only on that issued SF30 attachment and the eOffer listing. It does not use GSA Interact advance-notice drafts, vendor commentary, or other unofficial summaries of Refresh 34.

The next paragraph is the rule for existing orders and new ones:

The SF30 does not say that already-issued orders are retroactively rewritten. It also does not, in the passage opened for this report, identify a separately dated mass-modification instrument beyond the Refresh 34 SF30 attachment itself.

The 20-year duration limit is removed, and schedule clauses are consolidated

Under “GSA Acquisition Regulation (GSAR) Overhaul Wave 4 (Final) Updates,” the SF30 describes GSAR Part 538 modernization “through ‘evergreen contracting.’” The first listed effort is: “Removal of the 20 year limitation on contract duration (see 552.238-116).”

The same paragraph records “Deletion of 41 GSAR provisions/clauses, removal of eight (8) FAS FSS clauses, and addition of five (5) new GSAR clauses (see 552.238-190 to -194),” which it describes as consolidating existing GSAR clauses and integrating FSS “homegrown” clauses. The SF30 then lists the remaining GSAR schedule clauses, including the revised option-to-extend clause at 552.238-116 and the five new clauses 552.238-190 through 552.238-194.

A note in the same section says this wave “did not make any revisions to FSS ordering procedures located in GSAR subpart 538.71.” Those revisions, it says, “were incorporated through MAS Refresh 30 and are currently being processed via rulemaking.”

The domestic-end-product certificate is optional, and not yet enforced

The SF30 incorporates GSA Class Deviation CD-2026-04 by adding GSAR clause 552.238-130, Federal Supply Schedule (FSS) Domestic End Product Certificate (September 2026). “Offerors and contractors on the Federal Supply Schedule contract vehicle are permitted, but not required, to certify that listed products are FSS domestic end products as defined in the clause.”

The clause defines an FSS domestic end product as a product offered on the firm’s MAS contract, manufactured in the United States, and meeting a component test: if the product does not consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel or both, the cost of U.S.-mined, produced, or manufactured components must exceed 75 percent of all component costs; if it does consist wholly or predominantly of iron or steel, the cost of foreign iron or steel must be less than 5 percent of the total cost of all components.

The certification “is a material representation of fact to the Government.” If it is later determined that the offeror or contractor “knowingly rendered an erroneous certification,” the government “may pursue any available remedies, including those under the False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. 3729, et. seq.) or the Administrative False Claims Act.” A dealer, distributor, or other reseller, “acting in good faith, may rely upon information provided by an original equipment manufacturer.”

Enforcement is deferred: “GSA is seeking clearance from OMB for information collections related to GSAR 552.238-130. Until clearance is received, contractors and offers may offer this certification, but GSA will not enforce compliance with clause requirements and or share the information received.”

LLM safeguarding clause 552.239-7001 is added to the schedule

Under GSAR Part 539, the SF30 describes “a new AI-related clause for use in GSA contracts, including Schedules, to protect Government Data and to mitigate other risks presented by LLM systems.” The key update listed is addition of GSAR clause 552.239-7001, Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems (GSAR Deviation) (September 2026).

The significant-changes summary does not, in the passage opened here, restate the full clause text. This report treats the issued SF30 as establishing that the clause is now part of the Refresh 34 MAS package. A prior GovConCEO report examined the clause text itself; that earlier report is cited only as a cross-reference, not as a source of new official facts about Refresh 34.

Nitrile-glove removals are described as an intent, not as a completed action

The SF30’s “Covered Nitrile Gloves Updates” say that, “Consistent with an OMB August 31, 2026 memorandum, GSA intends to move toward removing items from MAS contracts that fall within the scope of DHS’s Best-in-Class PPE Safety Stock contract for domestically manufactured nitrile gloves.” It recites current offerings as powder-free, medical-grade nitrile gloves in 4 mil and 5 mil blue varieties and a 6 mil black variety.

GSA “intends to engage directly with affected contractors to validate which items fall within scope and consider contractor feedback before proceeding with removal.” The issued text does not itself list items already removed.

Facilities: the HVAC Maintenance subgroup is removed

Under changes to specific large categories, the SF30 addresses Facilities SIN 561210FAC, Facilities Support Services: “Removing the subgroup and revising SIN Notes to eliminate HVAC maintenance services as a standalone offering.” It also records “SIN subgroup removal: HVAC Maintenance: Boilers, Chillers, Refrigeration, etc.”

The same note says GSA “will not award MAS contracts to offerors only offering service types, such as janitorial, plumbing, electrical, or HVAC maintenance, which can effectively be performed by contractors who provide Complete Facilities Maintenance.” It names Maintenance of Fire Alarm Systems and Maintenance of Fire Suppression Systems, and Elevator/Escalator Inspection Service and Elevator/Escalator Maintenance Service, among standalone services under the SIN.

HACS: the voluntary Incident Response subgroup phases out at the end of FY2027

For Information Technology SIN 54151HACS, Highly Adaptive Cybersecurity Services, the SF30 says GSA is “Phasing out the voluntary Incident Response subgroup at the end of Fiscal Year 2027.” Contractors currently under that subgroup “who do not hold the Incident Handling and Event Management (IHEM) subgroup may evaluate their eligibility and request placement in the IHEM subgroup, which incorporates Incident Response capabilities.”

The SF30 does not further define the last day of Fiscal Year 2027, and it does not say the Incident Response subgroup is already closed.

Execution

The issued SF30 is a solicitation refresh and mass-modification attachment, not legal advice, and not a substitute for reading the full Refresh 34 package and the modification as issued to a particular contract.

  • Calendar the 90-day acceptance rule from issuance of the modification. Use the SF30’s “no later than 90 days after the modification has been issued” language unless a later official document names a date.
  • Separate new orders from old ones. New task and delivery orders, including BPA calls, issued after the modification’s effective date take the Refresh 34 terms. Orders issued before that date keep the terms in effect at award.
  • Treat a 552.238-130 certification as a material representation if you make one. The clause is optional, and GSA says it will not enforce it until OMB clearance is received. The SF30 still describes a knowing erroneous certification as a basis for available remedies, including the False Claims Act.
  • Read 552.239-7001 if you offer LLM-related work on schedule. The SF30 adds the clause to GSA contracts, including Schedules. This report does not restate the clause line by line.

Watch next

  • The 90-day acceptance window measured from when “the modification has been issued.” The SF30 does not print an end date.
  • OMB clearance for the 552.238-130 information collection, after which the SF30 says GSA may enforce the clause and share the information received.
  • Any later GSA action that actually removes covered nitrile-glove items, after the contractor engagement the SF30 describes. No such removal list was opened in the issued SF30.
  • The end of Fiscal Year 2027 for the voluntary HACS Incident Response subgroup, and any later official instruction on IHEM placement.
