Agency Reorganizations and Your Contracts: Five Executive Orders in Force, a Budget Full of Proposals

Key takeaways

  • Separate orders from proposals. Five executive orders below are in effect as signed. The FY2027 reorganizations are Budget proposals that need appropriations or other action.
  • Buying is moving to GSA. EO 14240 directs consolidation of common goods and services procurement, about $490 billion a year, at GSA.
  • Cost-type contracts need a written justification. EO 14402 makes fixed price the default. Agency heads must approve non-fixed-price contracts above $100 million (War), $35 million (NASA), $25 million (DHS) and $10 million (other agencies).
  • Existing contracts were and are being reviewed. EO 14222 ordered a 30-day review to "terminate or modify" covered contracts. EO 14402 targets each agency's 10 largest non-fixed-price contracts.
  • Defense primes face a new clause. EO 14372 directs a contract provision barring buybacks and distributions during periods of underperformance.
  • The budget proposes structural moves. These include USDA hubs, wildland fire unified at Interior, CWMD into CISA, and career and technical education moved to Labor.

Scope note: This report covers actions documented in the Federal Register and the FY2027 Budget. It does not cover agency-level reductions in force or individual contract terminations, which we did not verify.

Findings

Ordered: procurement consolidation at GSA (EO 14240)

The order says the government spends "approximately $490 billion per year on Federal contracts for common goods and services." It sets three deadlines:

  • 60 days. Agency heads submit proposals to have GSA conduct domestic procurement of common goods and services, "where permitted by law."
  • 90 days. The GSA Administrator submits a comprehensive plan to OMB.
  • 30 days. Under 40 U.S.C. 11302(e), OMB "shall designate" GSA as executive agent for all governmentwide acquisition contracts for IT. GSA may "defer or decline" the designation to keep service continuous.

We did not verify whether OMB completed that designation, or the contents of GSA's plan.

Ordered: review, justify, terminate or modify (EO 14222)

EO 14222 required three things:

  • Payment justification. Each agency must build a system to record every payment under covered contracts and grants, with "a brief, written justification" from the approving employee.
  • Contract review. Agencies must review all covered contracts and grants and, where appropriate and lawful, "terminate or modify (including through renegotiation)" them. The deadline was 30 days.
  • Warrant freeze. Agencies were not to "issue or approve new contracting officer warrants during the review period" unless the agency head found it necessary.

DOGE Team Leads provide the Administrator of the U.S. DOGE Service with "a monthly informational report on contracting activities."

Ordered: fixed price by default (EO 14402)

EO 14402 cites "$120 billion obligated on cost-reimbursement consulting contracts alone" in FY2024. It makes fixed-price contracts "the default and preferred method of procurement." Its mechanics:

  • Written justification. Any non-fixed-price contract "must be justified in writing by the contracting officer to the agency head."
  • Agency head approval above thresholds. These are $100 million (War), $35 million (NASA), $25 million (DHS) and $10 million (all others). Approval may be delegated to non-career employees.
  • Restructure the top 10. Within 90 days, each agency was to seek to "modify, restructure, or renegotiate its 10 largest non-fixed-price contracts by dollar value." R&D and pre-production development for major systems are exempt.
  • Reporting and the FAR. Agency heads report semiannually to OMB. The Administrator for Federal Procurement Policy was to propose FAR amendments within 120 days.

Ordered: defense contractor capital allocation (EO 14372)

EO 14372 directs the Secretary of War to identify, within 30 days and on a continuing basis, defense contractors that are underperforming or not prioritizing government work, and that engaged in "any stock buy-back or corporate distribution" during that period.

Identified contractors get notice. Where lawful, they may submit a board-approved remediation plan within 15 days.

Within 60 days, future contracts and renewals were to include a provision barring buybacks and distributions during periods of underperformance. They must also say executive incentive pay will not be tied to short-term metrics "such as free cash flow."

Ordered: Education wind-down (EO 14242)

EO 14242 directs the Secretary of Education, "to the maximum extent appropriate and permitted by law," to "facilitate the closure of the Department of Education." It requires continued "effective and uninterrupted delivery of services, programs, and benefits." The FY2027 Budget describes Education as on a "path to elimination."

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Expect buyers to change. Under EO 14240, common-goods and IT GWAC work moves toward GSA vehicles. Being on a GSA vehicle matters more.
  • Price fixed when you can. Above the EO 14402 thresholds, a cost-type proposal needs agency head approval. Expect buyers to prefer fixed-price or performance-incentive structures.
  • Track the receiving office. In a transfer, such as OCTAE to DOL or CWMD to CISA, the contracting office and CO may change.

Compliance cost

  • Payment justifications. EO 14222's justification step can slow invoice approval. Keep deliverable evidence ready to attach.
  • Defense capital allocation. Read new DoD contract provisions on buybacks and distributions. Note the 15-day remediation window.

Execution

  • Large cost-type contracts. If you hold one of an agency's 10 largest, expect requests to restructure toward fixed price.
  • Termination and modification rights. Terminations for convenience follow your contract's clauses. Document costs from the start.

Watch next

  • FAR changes from EO 14402. Look for proposed amendments on contract type.
  • The GSA consolidation plan and IT GWAC designation. Their status was not verified for this report.
  • FY2027 appropriations. Budget reorganizations need enacted bills. FY2027 is on a CR through Dec 11, 2026.
