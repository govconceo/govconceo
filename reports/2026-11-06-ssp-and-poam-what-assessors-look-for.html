SSP and POA&M: What Assessors Look For, Per NIST 800-171A and the CMMC Rule

Key takeaways

  • No SSP, no assessment. Under 32 CFR 170.24, the absence of an up-to-date SSP means an assessment "could not be completed." The SSP (3.12.4) can never go on a CMMC POA&M.
  • Eight objectives for the SSP. NIST SP 800-171A breaks 3.12.4 into eight determinations, [a] through [h]. They include boundary, environment, implementation method, connections and a defined update frequency.
  • Three objectives for the plan of action. 800-171A asks whether deficiencies are identified, whether a plan is developed, and whether it is implemented (3.12.2[a] to [c]).
  • A POA&M is not a pass. 32 CFR 170.24 says a POA&M "is not a substitute for a completed requirement." Unimplemented requirements are scored NOT MET.
  • CMMC POA&Ms are narrow. A conditional Level 2 status needs a score of at least 80% of the maximum, only 1-point items (plus non-FIPS encryption), and closeout within 180 days.
  • DOJ cites missing plans. MORSECORP, Raytheon/Nightwing and ILC Dover settlements all included allegations or admissions about missing or outdated SSPs. Penn State's turned on plan-of-action dates.

Findings

The SSP is the document everything else is scored against

NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2 requirement 3.12.4 is to "develop, document, and periodically update system security plans." The plans must describe system boundaries, environments of operation, how requirements are implemented, and connections to other systems.

DFARS 252.204-7020 defines a Basic Assessment as one based on the contractor's review of its system security plan(s). The SPRS submission is organized by SSP, with the CAGE codes each plan covers.

What 800-171A asks of an SSP: eight determinations

NIST SP 800-171A sets out the assessment objectives for 3.12.4. An assessor determines whether:

  • [a] Exists. A system security plan is developed.
  • [b] Boundary. The system boundary is described and documented.
  • [c] Environment. The system environment of operation is described and documented.
  • [d] Non-applicable items. Requirements approved by the designated authority as non-applicable are identified.
  • [e] Implementation. The method of implementing each requirement is described and documented.
  • [f] Connections. Relationships with or connections to other systems are described and documented.
  • [g] Frequency defined. The frequency for updating the plan is defined.
  • [h] Frequency met. The plan is updated at that frequency.

800-171A lists three methods: examine (for example, the plan and records of its reviews), interview (personnel with planning duties) and test (the processes for developing, reviewing and approving the plan).

Format is flexible; content is not

The Rev 2 discussion section for 3.12.4 gives organizations latitude on form:

The same discussion says security plans "need not be single documents" and can be a collection of existing documents. 32 CFR 170.4 defines the SSP as "the formal document" that describes components, environment, implementation and connections.

What 800-171A asks of a plan of action: three determinations

Requirement 3.12.2 is to "develop and implement plans of action designed to correct deficiencies and reduce or eliminate vulnerabilities." 800-171A asks whether:

  • [a] Identified. Deficiencies and vulnerabilities to be addressed are identified.
  • [b] Developed. A plan of action is developed to correct them.
  • [c] Implemented. The plan is implemented.

The Rev 2 discussion says organizations can document the SSP and plan of action "as separate or combined documents and in any chosen format."

Evidence has to be final

32 CFR 170.24(b)(1) sets the evidence standard for a MET finding. "All evidence must be in final form and not draft."

The rule lists working papers, drafts, and unofficial or unapproved policies as unacceptable forms of evidence. For each NOT MET objective, the assessor documents why the evidence does not conform.

Two items can still score MET under 170.24(b)(1):

  • Enduring exceptions. These count as MET when described, with any mitigations, in the SSP.
  • Temporary deficiencies. These count as MET when addressed in operational plans of action that include deficiency reviews and show progress.

The CMMC POA&M is a narrow, time-boxed tool

32 CFR 170.21 controls when a POA&M can support a Conditional CMMC status:

  • Level 1. No POA&M is permitted.
  • 80% threshold. For Level 2, the score divided by the number of Level 2 requirements must be at least 0.8.
  • 1-point items only. No POA&M item may carry more than 1 point. The one exception is 3.13.11 encryption that is used but not FIPS-validated, which is worth 3.
  • Never on a POA&M. 3.1.20, 3.1.22, 3.12.4 (the SSP), 3.10.3, 3.10.4 and 3.10.5.
  • 180 days. A closeout assessment must confirm closure within 180 days of the Conditional status date, or the status expires.

Under 170.24(c)(2)(i)(B)(6), "a POA&M addressing NOT MET security requirements is not a substitute for a completed requirement."

What DOJ settlements say about plans

Plan failures appear repeatedly in DOJ's cyber-fraud releases:

  • MORSECORP (Mar 2025, $4.6M). The company admitted it lacked a consolidated written plan for each covered system from January 2018 to January 2021.
  • Raytheon/Nightwing (May 2025, $8.4M). DOJ alleged a failure to develop and implement an SSP for an internal development system used on DoD work.
  • Penn State (Oct 2024, $1.25M). DOJ alleged the university misrepresented implementation dates and did not pursue its plans of action.
  • ILC Dover (Nov 2026, $13M). DOJ alleged no SSP, developed and periodically updated, for long stretches between 2018 and 2024, and gaps in plans of action and milestones.

These are allegations resolved by settlement unless the release says the company admitted facts, as MORSECORP did.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Treat the SSP as a proposal asset. The SPRS score that gates award (DFARS 252.204-7019) is built on it.
  • Scope matters. One SSP per system, with the CAGE codes it covers, is the unit SPRS records.

Compliance cost

  • Budget for upkeep, not authorship. Objectives [g] and [h] score whether you set an update frequency and kept to it.
  • Retire drafts. Draft policies are not acceptable evidence under 170.24(b)(1).
  • Fix the big items first. Under 170.21, 3-point and 5-point gaps cannot support a Conditional CMMC status, except non-FIPS encryption.

Execution

  • Date discipline. Plan dates feed the "score of 110" date in SPRS. Penn State's case shows those dates are treated as representations.
  • Name an owner. CMMC requires an Affirming Official, defined in 32 CFR 170.4, to affirm continuing compliance annually.
  • Map connections. Objective 3.12.4[f] and the cloud duties in 7012 both depend on knowing every external connection.

Watch next

  • CMMC Phase 2 review. Only Level 1 (Self) and Level 2 (Self) may be required while the July 13, 2026 suspension stands.
  • Rev 3. NIST shows Rev 2 withdrawn and superseded by Rev 3 (May 14, 2024). The DFARS and CMMC texts opened still cite Rev 2.
  • FAR CUI clause. Proposed FAR 52.240-7 (FAR Case 2026-001) would add a governmentwide CUI clause. It is not final.
