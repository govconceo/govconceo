Reading Agency Procurement Forecasts: What They Promise, What They Don't, and How Agencies Publish Them

Key takeaways

  • Forecasts are planning tools, not commitments. GSA says its governmentwide FCO tool "is for information and planning purposes only" and "does not commit the government to purchase."
  • The legal basis agencies cite is a 1988 law. Interior and State both trace their forecasts to Public Law 100-656, which requires agencies to publish projections of opportunities that small firms may be able to perform.
  • Acquisition.gov lists 22 agency forecast pages. Publishing practices differ widely across them.
  • Update schedules differ. DOE updates its HQ forecast monthly (latest Oct 6, 2026). State posted a mid-year FY2026 update on Aug 20, 2026. Interior's page still shows its FY2025 forecast.
  • The solicitation is what counts. GSA says final decisions on competition, small business participation and value are made "if and when a solicitation is posted to SAM.gov."

Scope note: This report is narrowed to sources we could open. Forecast pages for DoD, DOT, DOL, SSA, NRC, USDA and Commerce blocked automated access and are not described here.

Findings

Why forecasts exist

Both departments say their forecasts project anticipated contracts above the simplified acquisition threshold. These are contracts that small, small disadvantaged, women-owned, HUBZone and service-disabled veteran-owned small businesses "may be able to perform." That covers prime contracts and subcontracting opportunities.

We did not open the codified statutory text. The description above is the agencies' own.

The governmentwide FCO tool

GSA describes the Forecast of Contracting Opportunities (FCO) tool as "a governmentwide planning tool that shows upcoming federal contract opportunities." Many agencies use it, and GSA says it can surface opportunities "sometimes months before a solicitation appears on SAM.gov."

Each entry includes:

  • Status and timing. Award status and estimated award date.
  • What and where. Place of performance and NAICS code.
  • How it will be bought. Acquisition strategy, including whether a small business set-aside is planned, and contract type.
  • Who to call. A point of contact for each opportunity.

You do not need to register to use the tool. GSA recommends registering in SAM.gov before pursuing any opportunity that is solicited.

What GSA says forecasts do not do

GSA's disclaimers are explicit:

  • No commitment. The tool "does not commit the government to purchase."
  • Subject to change. "Forecasts can and do change so all opportunities in the FCO tool are subject to revision or cancellation."
  • Final terms come later. Decisions on "the extent of competition, small business participation, estimated value, or any other aspect of the procurement are made if and when a solicitation is posted to SAM.gov."

GSA's advice is to check both the FCO tool and SAM.gov regularly.

State says its updated version "may differ from the original forecast in both format and content." Read the most recent version, not the first one posted.

DOE: most dollars flow through contractors

DOE says "approximately 80% of DOE's annual procurement base is allocated to the Agency's Management and Operating Contractors (M&Os)," also called Facility Management Contractors. It lists procurement pages for M&O and FMC-managed sites such as Los Alamos, Sandia, Oak Ridge, Hanford and Savannah River.

For DOE, the federal forecast shows only part of the opportunity. The rest sits in contractor-run site procurement.

DOE also noted that its DOE/NNSA construction opportunities list was "currently being updated," and asked readers to check back in October 2026.

Interior's small business context

Interior says that in FY2023 it awarded 60% of its eligible prime contract dollars to small businesses. As of Nov 10, 2026, its page still links the FY2025 forecast, even though it states an October update cycle.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

  • Start with the FCO tool, then go to agency pages. Filter by NAICS, set-aside and estimated award date, then confirm on the agency's own page.
  • Contact the listed POC early. GSA suggests sending an expression of interest, a short capabilities statement with past performance, and questions about acquisition strategy.
  • For DOE, look at M&O sites. About 80% of the procurement base runs through them.

Compliance cost

  • Low cost, but discount for uncertainty. A forecast entry is not a requirement. Don't spend capture money on it without a sources-sought notice, draft RFP or other signal.

Execution

  • Check the date on every page. Interior's FY2025 forecast and State's mid-year update show how stale or revised a forecast can be.
  • Track the forecast into SAM.gov. Competition type and value are fixed only at solicitation.

Watch next

  • FY2027 forecasts. Interior and State both describe updates at the start of the fiscal year.
  • DOE's construction list. DOE said it would return in October 2026.
  • CR effects. Under P.L. 119-103, new starts cannot begin until full-year funding or an anomaly allows them.
