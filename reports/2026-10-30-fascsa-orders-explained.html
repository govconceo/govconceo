FASCSA Exclusion Orders Explained: Two Are Now Public, and Contractors Must Check SAM Every Three Months

Key takeaways

  • Two orders are public. DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 covers DeepSeek (released July 24, 2026). Order #2026-02 covers Acronis (released July 31, 2026). Their Federal Register notices appeared October 20 and October 30, 2026.
  • They reach contractors. FASCSA prohibitions cover contractor use of covered articles in performance. The Acronis order’s removal duty extends to information systems run by a contractor on an agency’s behalf.
  • Check SAM quarterly. FAR 52.204-30 requires contractors to search SAM.gov for “FASCSA order” at least once every three months during performance. A hit triggers a reasonable inquiry and a report within 3 business days.
  • New orders need a modification. An order issued after the solicitation applies to the contract only once it is added by amendment or modification. The quarterly check and reporting duty still apply.
  • Acronis deadline. Agencies must remove Acronis covered articles within 180 calendar days of the order, which is January 27, 2027, unless they obtain an exception.

Findings

An order is a targeted ban issued by one of three officials

The Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 created the Federal Acquisition Security Council. Officials can issue orders that exclude named sources or covered articles from procurements, remove them from agency systems, or both.

FAR 4.2301 names the three issuing officials. Which one matters depends on who awarded your contract.

  • DHS orders. Issued by the Secretary of Homeland Security for civilian agencies.
  • DoD orders. Issued by the Secretary of Defense for DoD and national security systems other than sensitive compartmented information systems.
  • DNI orders. Issued by the Director of National Intelligence for the intelligence community and sensitive compartmented information systems.
  • Default rule. Under FAR 4.2304(a)(1), civilian agency contracts are subject to DHS orders and DoD contracts to DoD orders, unless the program office instructs otherwise.

“Covered article” is broad. It includes IT and cloud services, telecommunications equipment and services, CUI processing, and hardware or software with embedded IT.

Two orders have been published so far

A Federal Register searched on October 30, 2026 returned two FASCSA exclusion and removal order notices, both from DHS. Both apply to civilian executive branch agencies, except national security systems and SCI systems.

  • #2026-01, DeepSeek. Agencies must exclude DeepSeek, its affiliates and their products from procurements immediately. They must also remove DeepSeek products and “third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations” from civilian agency IT immediately, or seek an exception.
  • Third-party reach. A footnote to the DeepSeek order says the order includes “third party implementations of DeepSeek used to provide products and services to civilian agencies.”
  • #2026-02, Acronis. Agencies must exclude Acronis AG, its affiliates and covered articles they supply, directly or indirectly, from all ongoing and future procurements, effective immediately.

A footnote to the Acronis order says it applies to all federal information systems, “including information systems used or operated by an agency or by a contractor of an agency.” Another footnote says the FAR’s term “waiver” means the same as the order’s “exception.”

What this report could not confirm

This report confirmed orders through their Federal Register notices. It did not confirm the SAM.gov listing, or whether any DoD or DNI orders exist.

FAR 4.2303(c) says orders are found in SAM. It also says some orders will not be in SAM and will appear only in the solicitation. Contractors therefore cannot rely on the Federal Register alone.

Two FAR clauses carry the obligations

FAR 52.204-29 is the solicitation provision and 52.204-30 is the contract clause. Both date from December 2023.

At the offer stage, the offeror must search SAM for “FASCSA order,” review the solicitation for orders not in SAM, and represent whether it will provide or use a covered article. If it will, the offeror must make detailed disclosures for each item.

  • Prohibition. Unless a waiver applies, contractors may not provide or use in performance any covered article, or product or service from a source, prohibited by an applicable order.
  • Timing. An order issued after the solicitation date applies only if added by amendment or modification, under 52.204-30(b)(4).
  • Waiver path. If a new order is added by modification, the contractor may seek a waiver by disclosing product, source, vendor, brand, model and reason for use.

The quarterly check is the core ongoing duty

The clause’s monitoring duty does not wait for a modification. It requires active checking throughout performance.

  • Reasonable inquiry. If a new order could affect your supply chain, check whether a covered item was provided or used. FAR 4.2301 says this does not require an internal or third-party audit.
  • 3 business days. Report contract and order numbers, product, source, vendor, brand, model and any mitigation. DoD contractors report at dibnet.dod.mil and others report to the contracting officer.
  • 10 business days. Follow up with mitigation and the steps taken to prevent recurrence.
  • Flowdown. The clause flows to all subcontracts, including commercial ones, except the quarterly SAM check in (c)(1).

Schedules and GWACs handle orders differently

For Federal Supply Schedules, GWACs and multi-agency contracts, FAR 4.2304(b) lets the awarding agency apply orders at the basic contract level. That is the preferred method, or the agency can apply them at the order level.

Under 52.204-30(d), schedule and GWAC holders must promptly remove prohibited products when the contracting officer notifies them. The pending FAR overhaul would make basic-contract application mandatory.

The FAR overhaul would consolidate the clause

The Revolutionary FAR Overhaul proposed rule for Parts 1, 2, 4, 33, 39, 40 and 53 (FAR Case 2026-001, which also amends Part 52) was published June 23, 2026. Comments closed July 23, 2026.

  • One clause. FASCSA, Section 889, Kaspersky, ByteDance and other prohibitions would move into new FAR Part 40, with provision 52.240-2 and clause 52.240-3, “Security Prohibitions and Exclusions.”
  • One clock. Reporting would be standardized to 72 hours from discovery, with one report.
  • One standard. A single reasonable-inquiry standard would confirm that no internal or third-party audit is required.

These are proposals. Until a final rule, 52.204-29 and 52.204-30 govern.

What it means for contractors

Bid strategy

Check SAM and every solicitation for orders before you represent under 52.204-29. Orders may apply through the solicitation even if they are not in SAM.

  • AI offerings. Confirm no DeepSeek model, and no product built on a DeepSeek implementation, sits in anything offered to civilian agencies.
  • Backup and recovery. Confirm Acronis software is not in offered solutions or in systems you operate for an agency.

Compliance cost

The fixed cost is a recurring SAM check and a software and component inventory good enough to support a reasonable inquiry. The variable cost is replacing an excluded product mid-contract.

  • Inventory first. You cannot complete a 3-business-day report without knowing where a product is deployed.
  • Subcontract visibility. Subcontractors must report to you, which only works if the clause is flowed down and they know what to look for.

Execution

For Acronis, the removal clock is the agency’s, but contractor-operated systems are in scope. Plan replacement before January 27, 2027, unless the agency tells you it holds an exception.

  • Calendar it. Set a recurring quarterly SAM review and keep evidence that it was done.
  • Watch for modifications. New orders reach your contract through modifications. Review them for waiver options within the process in 52.204-30(b)(5).

Watch next

  • Jan 27, 2027. Acronis removal deadline for agencies without an exception.
  • Final Part 40 rule. A final RFO rule would replace 52.204-29 and 52.204-30 with 52.240-2 and 52.240-3 and move reporting to 72 hours.
  • New orders. Check SAM.gov for the phrase “FASCSA order,” and the Federal Register for DHS notices.
